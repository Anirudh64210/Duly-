# Attorney network: what the law allows Duly to earn

2026-10-01. Part of the Duly cost and pricing pack, and the legal companion to channels.md section 4 (BACKLOG B10). The founder's idea: students get discounted access to immigration attorneys, Duly takes a cut of the fee, and law firms pay to stay on Duly. This file says, for each way of earning, what is allowed, under what structure, and what can go wrong.

**This is research for the founder and for counsel. It is not legal advice.** Every rule below binds the lawyer in the state where the lawyer is licensed, and several states also bind the platform directly. Counsel must confirm before anything is built or sold.

How to read it:
- Every source has its URL. "Fetched 2026-10-01" means fetched for this file. Quotes are verbatim from the fetched text.
- **(unverified)** marks something this pass could not read at the source (the ABA site refused every fetch with HTTP 403, and some state pages returned 404). It is from secondary text or general knowledge and must be checked.
- **(estimate)** marks a number worked out here.
- Founder rules that bear on this: BOUNDARY.md step back item 4 ("a neutral pointer such as the AILA lawyer search, no paid referrals"), BOUNDARY "Words Duly never uses about itself" (lawyer, attorney, legal advice ...), DECISIONS D14 (no attorney retained) and D28. Building B10 in any form changes BOUNDARY item 4. That is a founder decision, not this file's.

## 0. The answer on one page

1. **A percentage cut of an independent lawyer's fee is not allowed for Duly as an ordinary company, in any of the six states checked.** It is fee sharing with a nonlawyer (Model Rule 5.4(a)) and a payment for a recommendation (Model Rule 7.2(b)). Every Avvo opinion from 2016 and 2017 (Ohio, South Carolina, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, New York) struck down exactly this model, including when it was called a "marketing fee" and collected in a separate transaction. Florida's rule comment says it in one line: "A fee calculated as a percentage of the fee received by a lawyer ... would be an improper division of fees." Illinois, the most open state, also bars fees "calculated as a percentage of the lawyer's anticipated or actual legal fees". California has since 2026-01-01 let **any person** sue an uncertified referral service for $5,000 to $100,000 per violation. In Texas, taking money to solicit legal work can be the crime of barratry.
2. **A flat subscription from firms ("pay to stay on Duly") is allowed in most states as advertising,** if Duly does not recommend, rank by payment, vouch for or match lawyers to a student's case, and every firm pays the same fee whether or not it gets clients. Florida and Illinois add paperwork (Florida: Duly is a "qualifying provider" with 12 conditions; Illinois: an "intermediary connecting service" with disclosure and certification duties). California and Texas allow it only as **joint advertising**: the student must pick the lawyer from named listings, and in California every firm must sign an agreement taking liability for the ad's content. If Duly picks or suggests a lawyer using the student's case, it becomes a referral service that needs state certification.
3. **"Discounted rates for students" is allowed when the firm sets and offers the discount and Duly only displays it.** Duly must not set the lawyer's fee (Illinois says so outright). The discount has to be off a real regular price (FTC Guides, 16 CFR 233.1), and the lawyer cannot charge a Duly student more than the advertised price.
4. **There are three lawful ways to earn more than a flat listing fee, and each turns Duly into a different company:**
   - **Own a law firm through an Arizona Alternative Business Structure (ABS).** This is the only way to earn from legal fees themselves. Boundless, an immigration company, already does it ("Boundless Legal LLC is authorized and licensed by the Arizona Supreme Court"). It costs **$9,000 to apply and $6,000 a year** in state fees, plus an Arizona-licensed compliance lawyer, malpractice cover and a 7 to 9 month wait. Duly would then be the law firm's owner, which is the opposite of the current BOUNDARY.
   - **Run a legal service plan** (the LegalShield, LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer model). Students pay Duly a subscription, Duly pays the firms, and firms give plan members a discount. The money flows from Duly to the lawyers, not the other way, so it is not fee sharing (New Jersey 2017). But plans must register in some states, and some states regulate them as insurance.
   - **Become a certified lawyer referral service.** Certified or bar-approved services may charge percentage fees in some states, but certification is state by state, often limited to bar or nonprofit operators, and capped. Texas caps what the client pays for the first 30 minutes at $20. Not a practical path for Duly **(estimate)**.
5. **Utah's sandbox is closed to Duly.** It stopped taking ABS-only entities at the end of 2024, its last application deadline was 2026-05-29, and it sunsets on 2027-08-14.
6. **Recommendation (for the founder and counsel to decide):** if B10 goes ahead, start with a flat-fee, student-driven directory, built to the strictest state rules (section 7.1). Revenue at that stage is the $36k to $300k a year **(estimate)** in channels.md 4.3. Look at an Arizona ABS only if the founder wants Duly to become a legal provider. That is a separate company, brand and budget, and it reverses BOUNDARY. Do not build a percentage cut on independent firms in any form.

## 1. The two rules every model has to pass

### 1.1 Fee sharing: Model Rule 5.4

The ABA's own pages refused the fetch (HTTP 403, 2026-10-01). The text below is Illinois's adoption, which uses the Model Rule's words for these parts ([Illinois Rule 5.4](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/890bf990-662c-47b4-ab8d-d962d09ee740/file), fetched 2026-10-01):

> "(a) A lawyer or law firm shall not share legal fees with a nonlawyer, except that: ..." [the exceptions cover a deceased lawyer's estate, buying a practice, employee retirement plans, and court-awarded fees shared with a nonprofit]
>
> "(c) A lawyer shall not permit a person who recommends, employs, or pays the lawyer to render legal services for another to direct or regulate the lawyer's professional judgment in rendering such legal services."
>
> "(d) A lawyer shall not practice with or in the form of a professional corporation or association authorized to practice law for a profit, if: (1) a nonlawyer owns any interest therein ..."

What it means for Duly: no exception covers a platform. Any payment that rises or falls with the lawyer's fee is the risk. Paragraph (d) also stops Duly from owning a law firm, except in Arizona (section 3).

### 1.2 Paying for recommendations: Model Rule 7.2(b)

The 2018 Model Rule text, as summarised from memory **(unverified; ABA site refused)**: a lawyer shall not "compensate, give or promise anything of value to a person for recommending the lawyer's services", except to (1) "pay the reasonable costs of advertisements or communications permitted by this Rule"; (2) "pay the usual charges of a legal service plan or a not-for-profit or qualified lawyer referral service"; (3) buy a law practice; (4) make non-exclusive reciprocal referral agreements; (5) give nominal thank-you gifts.

North Carolina's adoption (fetched 2026-10-01, [NC State Bar Rule 7.2](https://www.ncbar.gov/for-lawyers/ethics-and-governing-rules/rules-of-professional-conduct/71-76-information-about-legal-services/72-communications-concerning-a-lawyers-services-specific-rules/)) carries the Model comments that matter most:

> "[2] ... A communication contains a recommendation if it endorses or vouches for a lawyer's credentials, abilities, competence, character, or other professional qualities. Directory listings and group advertisements that list lawyers by practice area, without more, do not constitute impermissible 'recommendations.'"
>
> "[5] A lawyer may pay others for generating client leads, such as Internet-based client leads, as long as the lead generator does not recommend the lawyer, any payment to the lead generator is consistent with Rules 1.5(e) (division of fees) and 5.4 (professional independence of the lawyer), and the lead generator's communications are consistent with Rule 7.1 ... a lawyer must not pay a lead generator that states, implies, or creates a reasonable impression that it is recommending the lawyer, is making the referral without payment from the lawyer, or has analyzed a person's legal problems when determining which lawyer should receive the referral."

**The last clause is the hardest one for Duly.** Duly's product is an analysis of the student's case. Any feature that uses that case to choose, rank or suggest a lawyer looks like "analyzed a person's legal problems when determining which lawyer should receive the referral". The safe line: the student searches and chooses, and Duly does not route.

What the three exceptions mean:
- **Advertising costs.** A fixed price for a fixed period, the same whether or not clients come. Pennsylvania: "The cost of advertising does not vary depending upon whether the advertising succeeded in bringing in business, or on the amount of revenue generated by a matter" (quoted in NJ Joint Opinion 732, section 2).
- **Qualified lawyer referral service.** The Model comment, as Texas reproduces it: "Qualified referral services are consumer-oriented organizations that provide unbiased referrals to lawyers with appropriate experience in the subject matter of the representation and afford other client protections, such as complaint procedures or malpractice insurance requirements" ([Texas Disciplinary Rules, Rule 7.03 comment 14](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Home&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=27271), version "Including Amendments Effective January 31, 2022", fetched 2026-10-01). "Qualified" generally means approved by a state authority. Many states approve only bar-run or nonprofit services **(unverified as a general rule; state detail in section 4)**.
- **Legal service plan.** "A prepaid or group legal service plan or a similar delivery system that assists people who seek to secure legal representation" (same Texas comment 14). See section 5.

### 1.3 Federal immigration practice does not remove the state rules

- Any US lawyer can represent a student before USCIS. 8 CFR 1.2: "Attorney means any person who is eligible to practice law in, and is a member in good standing of the bar of, the highest court of any State ..." ([eCFR 8 CFR 1.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-1.2), fetched 2026-10-01). So a Duly network will hold lawyers from many states, and **each lawyer's home-state rules apply to that lawyer**. The network has to meet the strictest state among its lawyers **(estimate, standard reading; confirm under each state's Rule 8.5)**.
- Immigration practitioners also answer to federal conduct rules. 8 CFR 1003.102(d) bars soliciting "through in-person or live telephone contact or through the use of runners ... when a significant motive for the practitioner's doing so is the practitioner's pecuniary gain", and 1003.102(f) bars "a false or misleading communication about his or her qualifications or services" ([eCFR 8 CFR 1003.102](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-1003.102), fetched 2026-10-01). These rules contain no fee-sharing clause, so the state rules decide that question.

## 2. The Avvo opinions (2016 to 2017): the closest precedent

Avvo Legal Services sold fixed-price legal services, including "a green card application", which is immigration work. The client paid Avvo. Avvo paid the lawyer and then, in a separate transaction, withdrew a "marketing fee" scaled to the price of the service. New York's description: "a $149 document review service results in a $40 marketing fee", with fees "from $10 (for $39 services) to $400 (for $2,995 services)" ([NYSBA Opinion 1132](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1132/), fetched 2026-10-01).

Every state that ruled said lawyers could not take part. The model sounds like the founder's "cut of the fee" with a different label.

| State, opinion, date | Holding | Key reasoning (quoted) |
|---|---|---|
| Ohio, Board of Professional Conduct Op. 2016-3, 2016-06-03 | Referral fee and fee splitting | "Unlike advertising fees that are fixed amounts and paid for a fixed period of time, these 'marketing fees' are a percentage of the fee generated on each legal service completed by the lawyer. Therefore, a fee-splitting arrangement that is dependent on the number of clients obtained or the legal fee earned does not comport with the Rules of Professional Conduct." |
| South Carolina, Ethics Advisory Op. 16-06, 2016-07-14 | Violates 5.4(a) and 7.2(c) | "A lawyer cannot do indirectly what would be prohibited if done directly." The fee "amount[s] to a contingency advertising fee arrangement rather than a cost that can be assessed for reasonableness by looking at market rate or comparable services." |
| Pennsylvania Bar Assn., Formal Op. 2016-200, September 2016 | Fee sharing; not an advertising cost | "The manner in which the payments are structured is not dispositive of whether the lawyer's payment to the Business constitutes fee sharing." |
| New Jersey, ACPE Joint Op. 732 / CAA 44 / UPL 54, 2017-06-21 | Avvo: 5.4(a), 7.2(c) and 7.3(d) violated. LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer: legal service plans, allowed only if registered | "The label Avvo assigns to this payment ('marketing fee') does not determine the purpose of the fee." "Sharing fees with a nonlawyer is prohibited, without qualification." "Avvo charges a pay-per-legal-service fee, which is a hallmark of an attorney referral service." |
| New York State Bar, Op. 1132, 2017-08-08 | Improper payment for a recommendation under 7.2(a); did not decide 5.4 | Avvo "effectively 'vouches for' each participating lawyer's credentials, abilities, and competence by offering a full refund if the client is not satisfied." "A lawyer paying Avvo's current marketing fee for Avvo Legal Services is making an improper payment for a recommendation in violation of Rule 7.2(a)." |

Sources: the Ohio, South Carolina and Pennsylvania quotes are as quoted inside the New Jersey opinion ([NJ Joint Opinion 732, as linked by the ABA Journal](https://www.dropbox.com/s/5plgfqgi26zuym1/ACPE%20732%20Avvo%2C%20LegalZoom%2C%20Rocket%20Lawyer%206.21.17.pdf?dl=0), fetched 2026-10-01; link found in [ABA Journal, 2017](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/avvo_violates_nj_ethics_rules_banning_fee_sharing_lawyer_referral_payments/)). The original Ohio, South Carolina and Pennsylvania pages returned 404 in this pass **(original texts unverified)**. New York: [NYSBA 1132](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1132/), fetched 2026-10-01.

What the opinions teach Duly:
1. **Labels and timing do not help.** A "platform fee", "marketing fee" or "technology fee" taken in a second transaction is still fee sharing if it tracks the legal fee.
2. **A flat fee per lead is treated differently from a fee per retained client.** NJ: "Lawyers may pay a set, flat amount for potential client inquiries or 'leads' that may or may not result in retention of a client for a specific matter, but they may not pay a fee in exchange for referral or retention of a client for a specific case." (citing NJ CAA Opinion 43, June 2011).
3. **Guarantees and ratings count as vouching** (NY 1132). A "Duly-vetted", "Duly guarantee" or "Top attorney" badge on a paid listing turns advertising into a paid recommendation.
4. **Subscription plans passed where Avvo failed.** NJ found LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer's monthly fees "do not violate this Rule. Those monthly subscription fees are not paid to the lawyers providing the service; the lawyers have not shared their legal fees." Their only problem was not registering (section 5).
5. **Avvo did not change the rule by arguing access to justice or the First Amendment.** NJ: "The First Amendment does not protect lawyers who seek to participate in prohibited attorney referral programs or engage in impermissible fee sharing."
6. Contrast: **group-coupon ("deal of the day") sites** were allowed in New York in 2011. There the site's percentage was treated as "the reasonable cost of advertisements" because "The website has not taken any action to refer a potential client to a particular lawyer – instead it has carried a particular lawyer's advertising message" ([NYSBA Opinion 897, 2011-12-13](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-897/), fetched 2026-10-01). The ABA reached a similar view in Formal Opinion 465 (2013) **(unverified; ABA site refused)**. Do not build on this. Six years later New York itself rejected the Avvo percentage, and a Duly offer chosen from inside the student's case is much closer to Avvo than to a coupon. Avvo Legal Services was shut down in 2018 after Internet Brands bought Avvo **(shutdown unverified; the acquisition is reported by the [Seattle Times, 2018-01-11](https://www.seattletimes.com/business/technology/seattle-legal-company-avvo-to-be-bought-by-internet-brands/) as cited on Wikipedia)**.

## 3. Owning the law firm: Arizona ABS, and Utah's closed sandbox

### 3.1 Arizona Alternative Business Structures

- **What it is.** "'Alternative business structure' ('ABS') is a business entity that includes nonlawyers who have an economic interest or Decision-making Authority in the firm and provides legal services in accord with Supreme Court Rules 31 and 31.1(c)" ([ACJA 7-209, March 2026 version](https://www.azcourts.gov/Portals/0/0/admcode/pdfcurrentcode/7-209%20Alternative%20Business%20Structures%2003_2026.pdf), fetched 2026-10-01). Arizona's court "eliminat[ed] ... Ethical Rule 5.4, which had previously barred nonlawyers from having an ownership interest in law firms and prohibited fee-sharing between lawyers and nonlawyers", effective 2021 ([ABS Committee 2025 Annual Report, dated 2026-04-27](https://www.azcourts.gov/Portals/0/2025_Committee%20on%20Alternative%20Business%20Structure%20Annual%20Report.pdf), fetched 2026-10-01).
- **Arizona also proposed deleting ER 7.2's ban on paying for referrals.** The 2019 task force: "The most significant proposed amendment ... would eliminate current ER 7.2(b)'s prohibition against giving anyone anything of 'value' for recommending a lawyer or referring a potential client to a lawyer" ([Arizona Task Force report](https://www.azcourts.gov/Portals/0/74/LSTF/Report/LSTFReportRecommendationsRED10042019.pdf), fetched 2026-10-01). **That the Court adopted this deletion is unverified in this pass.** Counsel should confirm it, because it decides whether Arizona-licensed lawyers may pay a platform per client.
- **Purpose test.** An applicant "must demonstrate how its licensure will advance" access to legal services, innovation in their delivery, investment capital, certain equity ownership, or "Multidisciplinary ownership for purposes of providing coordinated delivery of both legal and non-legal client services" (ACJA 7-209(E)(1)(a)). Since June 2025 applicants must also "describe how their ABS services will advance access to legal services" (2025 Annual Report).
- **Compliance lawyer.** The ABS must name one, who must be "an active member of the State Bar of Arizona in good standing", hold decision-making authority, serve no more than three entities, and "Proactively and persistently ensure that the ABS, its lawyers, and Authorized Persons comply with the Arizona Rules of Professional Conduct" (ACJA 7-209(A), (G)(3)). The state's FAQ: "At least one lawyer licensed to practice law in Arizona must be appointed by the ABS to serve as its compliance lawyer" ([Arizona ABS FAQ](https://www.azcourts.gov/accesstolegalservices/Questions-and-Answers/abs), fetched 2026-10-01).
- **State fees, ACJA 7-209(J), verbatim amounts:** initial application "Regular ABS $ 9,000.00" (Nonprofit $3,000, Equity $4,500). Annual fee "Regular ABS $ 6,000.00" (Nonprofit $2,000, Equity $3,000). Investigation costs over $1,500 or 80 hours of staff time are billed "at $100 per hour". A late renewal costs "$300.00 for each day".
- **Time.** "The average processing time has increased to approximately 7–9 months from the date of receipt" (2025-10-09). As of 2026-09-11, "Our online application portal is temporarily unavailable" ([ABS Announcements](https://www.azcourts.gov/cld/Alternative-Business-Structure/Announcements), fetched 2026-10-01). Discipline can include "A civil fine in an amount not to exceed $1,000,000" (ACJA 7-209).
- **Scale.** "As of December 31, 2025, the program oversaw 152 active licensed ABS entities." In 2025 the court approved 39 of 70 initial applications and denied 2 (2025 Annual Report).
- **Who has one** ([ABS Directory](https://www.azcourts.gov/cld/Alternative-Business-Structure/Directory), fetched 2026-10-01, 181 entries including inactive): "LZ Legal Services, LLC" (Active; LegalZoom's law firm **(link between the names unverified)**), "KPMG Law US, LLC" (Active), "Rocket Legal Professional Services, Inc." (Active), "Axiom Advice & Counsel LLC" (surrendered in good standing), and immigration firms including "Boundless Legal, LLC", "iVisa Legal, LLC", "Crimson Talent Immigration, LLC", "My Visa Source Legal Group Corp", "Globiliti Legal, LLC (dba VisaPlace Legal, LLC)", "Arizona Immigration & Legal Services (AILS Law Group)".
- **The closest analogue to Duly is Boundless.** Its site: "Boundless Legal LLC is authorized and licensed by the Arizona Supreme Court, license number 70276." "Attorneys at Boundless Legal LLC are licensed in the State of Arizona." "Boundless Legal LLC practices federal immigration law before United States Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS)" ([boundless.com](https://www.boundless.com/), fetched 2026-10-01). In other words, a tech company owns a federal immigration law firm staffed with Arizona-licensed lawyers and serves clients nationally. That works because 8 CFR 1.2 lets a lawyer from any state appear before USCIS.

What an ABS does and does not solve for Duly:
- **It solves:** Duly (or a sister company) can own the firm and keep its profit. That is legal "revenue from fees", but it is a law firm's profit, not a cut of other firms' fees.
- **It does not let Duly take a cut from independent firms in other states.** A New York or Texas lawyer is still bound by their own 5.4 and 7.2, whoever the payee is. California now bars it by statute. Since 2026-01-01, Business and Professions Code 6156 provides: "No attorney licensed or otherwise authorized to practice in this state shall share legal fees directly or indirectly with an out-of-state alternative business structure unless all of the following apply: (1) The attorney is also licensed in the state in which the alternative business structure is approved. (2) The fees are compensation for the provision of legal services in that state. (3) The law of that state is controlling pursuant to Rule 8.5 ..." The penalty is "Statutory damages of ten thousand dollars ($10,000) per violation or three times the actual damages", and the section lasts "until January 1, 2030". It does not apply to a contract with "a specific dollar amount for services rendered" where "No payment is related to the referral of legal services or purchase of a lead" and "No payment is contingent on the amount recovered" ([AB 931, Stats. 2025 ch. 565](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB931), fetched 2026-10-01).
- **It conflicts with BOUNDARY.** Duly would own a firm that gives legal advice, while Duly's own product must never conclude about a student. It needs a separate entity and brand, and a wall so the firm never steers Duly's product. Every ABS lawyer's advice also exposes the owner to malpractice and regulatory risk.
- **Cost (estimate).** State fees of $9k plus $6k a year. Add a compliance lawyer and at least one Arizona-licensed immigration lawyer (salary or contract), malpractice insurance (the ABS files a "Professional Liability Insurance Disclosure Form", [ABS Resources](https://www.azcourts.gov/cld/Alternative-Business-Structure/Resources)), Arizona counsel for the application ($15k to $50k, **estimate, not sourced**), and 7 to 9 months. Year-one cost is likely **$150k to $300k (estimate)** before any revenue.
- **Other states.** Some lawyers report that DC Rule 5.4(b) allows limited nonlawyer partners, and that other states have discussed reform **(unverified; not useful to Duly as a platform)**. ABA Formal Opinion 499 (2021) is reported to allow lawyers elsewhere a passive investment in an ABS **(unverified)**.

### 3.2 Utah's regulatory sandbox

- "The Utah legal regulatory sandbox is set to sunset on August 14, 2027." "The Court closed the alternative business structure (ABS)-only portion of the Sandbox at the end of 2024" ([Utah Office of Legal Services Innovation](https://utahinnovationoffice.org/), fetched 2026-10-01).
- Phase 3: "Application Cut-Off Date: 5:00PM MST on May 29, 2026." New proposals needed "at least a plausible connection to the regulatory reforms that will be permitted after the Sandbox sunsets" ([Phase 3](https://utahinnovationoffice.org/phase-3-implementation/), fetched 2026-10-01).
- Seven entities are currently authorized, some offering immigration help (1Law, Elysium Legal, Pearson Butler) ([Authorized entities](https://utahinnovationoffice.org/authorized-entities/), fetched 2026-10-01).
- **For Duly: not available.** It is past the deadline, it is closing, and it was built for services to Utah consumers.

## 4. State by state: the six main immigration-attorney states

All texts fetched 2026-10-01.

### 4.1 California (strictest on platforms)

- **Rule 7.2(b)** (effective 2018-11-01) allows a lawyer to "(1) pay the reasonable* costs of advertisements" and "(2) pay the usual charges of a legal services plan or a qualified lawyer referral service. A qualified lawyer referral service is a lawyer referral service established, sponsored and operated in accordance with the State Bar of California's Minimum Standards for a Lawyer Referral Service in California" ([State Bar of California, Rule 7.2](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/rules/Rule_7.2-Exec_Summary-Redline.pdf)). Comment [1] matters for discounts: an ad is misleading if it "sets forth a specific fee or range of fees for a particular service where, in fact, the lawyer charges or intends to charge a greater fee than that stated in the advertisement." (Later amendments to Rule 7.2 not checked, **unverified**.)
- **The statute binds the platform itself.** Business and Professions Code 6155(a): "An individual, partnership, corporation, association, or any other nongovernmental entity shall not operate for the direct or indirect purpose, in whole or in part, of referring potential clients to attorneys, and no attorney shall accept a referral of such potential clients, unless ... (1) The service is certified by the State Bar of California ... (2) The combined charges to the potential client by the referral service and the attorney ... do not exceed the total cost that the client would normally pay if no referral service were involved" ([BPC 6155](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=6155)).
- **Joint advertising is the directory safe harbour.** Under 6155(g), as amended by SB 37 (Stats. 2025 ch. 645, effective 2026-01-01): "(A) The advertisement identifies by name the advertising attorneys or law firms whom the consumer of legal services may select and initiate contact with. (B) Each participating attorney or law firm shall have executed a joint advertising agreement with the entity managing the joint advertising services, under which the attorney or law firm expressly takes liability for the content of the advertising." Whereas "(2) Certifiable referral activity involves ... some person or entity other than the consumer and advertising attorney or law firms which, in person, electronically, or otherwise, refers the consumer to an attorney or law firm not identified in the advertising."
- **New private right of action (SB 37, BPC 6156.5).** "Any person may bring a civil action for a violation of Section 6155 for ... Statutory damages of a minimum of five thousand dollars ($5,000) up to a maximum of one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000) per violation or three times the amount of actual damages, whichever is larger", plus "Attorney's fees". "The right of action under this section exists independently of any enforcement action or inaction by any governmental agency" ([SB 37](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB37)). The State Bar can also sue for civil penalties under 6155.1. **For Duly this is the largest single risk.** A matching feature used by many California students could add up to many violations.
- SB 37 also widened "advertisement" to "any communication ... that provides information concerning a lawyer or the lawyer's services for the purpose of encouraging individuals to secure the services of the lawyer", and banned references to awards or recognition from an organization that charges for them (BPC 6157 and following, same bill). **A paid "Duly Partner" badge is a risk under that rule.**
- Certified lawyer referral services must make every member lawyer carry errors and omissions cover of "not less than one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000) for each occurrence and three hundred thousand dollars ($300,000) aggregate" (6155(e)(6)). A service may not be owned by lawyers who get more than 20% of its referrals (6155(b)). The State Bar's minimum standards and certification fees were not readable in this pass **(unverified)**.
- **AB 931 / BPC 6156:** the ABS fee-sharing ban, quoted in section 3.1.

### 4.2 New York

- **Rule 7.2(a):** "A lawyer shall not compensate or give anything of value to a person or organization to recommend or obtain employment by a client, or as a reward for having made a recommendation resulting in employment by a client, except that: ... (2) a lawyer may pay the usual and reasonable fees or dues charged by a qualified legal assistance organization ..." ([22 NYCRR 1200.7.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/22-NYCRR-1200.7.2)).
- **Rule 7.2(b)** lists who may recommend lawyers: legal aid offices, military legal assistance, "(3) a lawyer referral service operated, sponsored or approved by a bar association or authorized by law or court rule", and "(4) any bona fide organization that recommends, furnishes or pays for legal services to its members or beneficiaries". Condition (vi) of (4) requires the organization to file "at least annually a report with respect to its legal service plan ... showing its terms, its schedule of benefits, its subscription charges, agreements with counsel and financial results."
- **Rule 5.4(a):** "A lawyer or law firm shall not share legal fees with a nonlawyer", with no platform exception ([22 NYCRR 1200.5.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/22-NYCRR-1200.5.4)).
- **Opinions:** 1132 (Avvo, 2017, banned) and 897 (group coupons, 2011, allowed), section 2.
- New York Judiciary Law sections 479 and 482 (solicitation, and employing a person to obtain business) were not fetched **(unverified)**.

### 4.3 Texas

- **Rule 5.04(a):** "A lawyer or law firm shall not share or promise to share legal fees with a non-lawyer, except ..." [estate and retirement exceptions only].
- **Rule 7.03(e):** "A lawyer shall not pay, give, or offer to pay or give anything of value to a person not licensed to practice law for soliciting or referring prospective clients for professional employment, except nominal gifts ... (1) This Rule does not prohibit a lawyer from paying reasonable fees for advertising and public relations services or the usual charges of a lawyer referral service that meets the requirements of Texas law." ([Texas Disciplinary Rules, 2022-01-31 version](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Home&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=27271). Later 2024 amendments not checked, **unverified**.)
- **Lawyer referral services need a state certificate.** "'Lawyer referral service' means a person or the service provided by the person that refers potential clients to lawyers regardless of whether the person uses the term 'referral service' to describe the service provided" (Occ. Code 952.002). "A person may not operate a lawyer referral service in this state unless the person holds a certificate" (952.101). Excluded from the definition: legal service organizations that meet the rules, "a plan of prepaid legal services insurance", and "lawyers who jointly advertise their services in a manner that clearly shows that the advertising is intended solely to solicit clients for those lawyers" (952.003). Fee cap: "The combined amounts of any fee charged to a potential client by the lawyer or the referral service may not exceed $20 for the first 30 minutes of the initial office visit with the lawyer" (952.155(b)) ([Tex. Occ. Code ch. 952 via Texas.Public.Law](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_952.002), mirror of statutes.capitol.texas.gov, fetched 2026-10-01).
- **Criminal exposure.** Penal Code 38.12(a): a person commits barratry if, "with intent to obtain an economic benefit", the person "(4) pays or gives or offers to pay or give a person money or anything of value to solicit employment" or "(6) accepts or agrees to accept money or anything of value to solicit employment". It is an exception if "the person's conduct is authorized by the Texas Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct" ([Tex. Penal Code 38.12](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._penal_code_section_38.12)). A percentage or per-client fee from Texas lawyers is not authorized, so the exception would not apply.

### 4.4 Florida (Duly would be a "qualifying provider" even as a plain directory)

- **Rule 4-7.17(b):** "A lawyer may not give anything of value to a person for recommending the lawyer's services, except that a lawyer may pay the reasonable cost of advertising permitted by these rules, may pay the usual charges of a lawyer referral service, lawyer directory, qualifying provider or other legal service organization ..." ([Rules Regulating The Florida Bar, ch. 4, October 2026 edition](https://www-media.floridabar.org/uploads/2026/10/2027_04-OCT-Chapter-4-RRTFB.pdf), fetched 2026-10-01).
- **Rule 4-7.22(b):** a qualifying provider is anyone "that receives any benefit or consideration, monetary or otherwise, for the direct or indirect referral of prospective clients to lawyers", including "(1) matching or other connecting of a prospective client to a lawyer", "(3) publishing in any media a listing of lawyers or law firms together in one place", and "(4) providing tips or leads". **A paid Duly directory qualifies.**
- **Rule 4-7.22(d)**, the conditions Duly would have to meet. Among them: "(2) receives no fee or charge that is a division or sharing of fees, unless the qualifying provider is The Florida Bar Lawyer Referral Service or a lawyer referral service approved by The Florida Bar"; "(5) provides The Florida Bar, on no less than an annual basis, with the names and Florida bar membership numbers of all lawyers participating"; "(8) neither represents nor implies to the public that the qualifying provider is endorsed or approved by The Florida Bar"; "(10) affirmatively discloses to the prospective client at the time a referral, match or other connection is made of the location of a bona fide office by city, town or county of the lawyer"; "(11) does not use a name or engage in any communication with the public that could lead prospective clients to reasonably conclude that the qualifying provider is a law firm or directly provides legal services"; "(12) has lawyers from at least 4 different law firms participating". Each lawyer "must report to The Florida Bar within 15 days of agreeing to participate" (4-7.22(e)).
- **Comment, explicit on a cut:** "A fee calculated as a percentage of the fee received by a lawyer, or based on the success or perceived value of the case, would be an improper division of fees." Also improper: when the provider "places limits on a lawyer's representation of a client, requires or prohibits the performance of particular legal services or tasks, or requires the use of particular forms or the use of particular third party providers". **Requiring firms to use Duly's case packet could fall under that last clause.** It must stay optional.
- **Rule 4-5.4(a)** follows the usual ban. It even forbids bonuses to nonlawyer employees "calculated as a percentage of legal fees".
- Legal expense insurance: see section 5.

### 4.5 Illinois (most open state; rules changed 2025-07-01)

- **Rule 7.2(c)** (amended 2025-04-01, effective 2025-07-01) lets a lawyer "accept a connection of a potential client through, and/or pay the usual charges of, an intermediary connecting service (ICS)" if the lawyer "discloses to the client the relationship between the lawyer and the ICS and, if applicable, whether the lawyer pays to the ICS a connecting fee", and takes reasonable steps so that, among other things: "(2) the ICS does not interfere with the attorney-client relationship ... including by unilaterally setting the fee the lawyer may charge the client"; "(7) if the ICS uses rating criteria of the lawyer, the ICS makes the criteria publicly available"; and, for non-bar services, "(8)(i) the charges or fees of the ICS are not contingent on the outcome of the matter; (ii) the usual charges that the ICS charges or collects are not calculated as a percentage of the lawyer's anticipated or actual legal fees; (iii) the ICS does not refer or recommend ... except that the ICS may permit reviews and ratings of lawyers and the ICS may offer a list of lawyers based upon a user-defined search from which the potential client can select a lawyer; and (iv) the ICS does not receive any funds a potential client pays for the lawyer's services ... except if the ICS uses a payment processing system that remits those funds to the lawyer at the time payment is collected" ([Illinois Rule 7.2](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/39c73f58-b094-4603-84a9-a590a1f17b97/file), fetched 2026-10-01).
- **Comment [7] permits a flat fee per hired client:** "The usual charges of an ICS may include paying the ICS for generating client leads or paying the ICS a reasonable connecting fee for every connection that results in a potential client hiring the lawyer". This is the opposite of New Jersey's per-retention rule. It is allowed only for Illinois lawyers and Illinois matters, and only if the fee is not a percentage. The ICS definition covers services "for the rendition of legal services in Illinois".
- Comment [8A]: "an ICS cannot unilaterally set the fee the lawyer may charge a client". Requiring exclusivity, or "prohibiting the lawyer from accepting clients outside of the ICS", is improper influence. An ICS "may require a lawyer to maintain malpractice insurance".
- Duly would need to give each Illinois lawyer the written certification the rule describes ("Sample RPC 7.2(c) Intermediary Connecting Services Certification Letter", [Illinois Article VIII](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/rules/supreme-court-rules?a=viii)).
- **Rule 5.4(a)** is the standard ban. Comment [7] to Rule 7.2 says paying an ICS's usual charges under 7.2(c) "does not constitute fee-splitting under 5.4(a)", and gives that latitude for percentage fees only to bar and legal-aid services.

### 4.6 New Jersey

- **RPC 7.2(c)** (as quoted in Joint Op. 732): a lawyer "may pay the usual charges of a not-for-profit lawyer referral service or other legal service organization". **RPC 7.3(d):** a lawyer may pay "the usual and reasonable fees or dues charged by a lawyer referral service operated, sponsored, or approved by a bar association". A for-profit referral service is therefore not available.
- **Legal service plans (RPC 7.3(e)(4)),** as summarised in Joint Op. 732: if the organization is for profit, the services cannot be rendered by lawyers "employed, directed, supervised or selected by it"; the member "must be recognized as the client of the lawyer"; the member must be able to choose other counsel; and "the organization must register its plan with the Supreme Court (Administrative Office of the Courts)".
- **Leads:** a flat fee per lead is allowed, a fee per retained client is not (CAA Op. 43, as quoted in Joint Op. 732).

## 5. Legal service plans (prepaid legal)

### 5.1 How LegalShield is built

From Pre-Paid Legal Services, Inc.'s last annual report before it was taken private (Form 10-K for 2010, filed 2011-02-28, [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/311657/000120677411000352/prepaid_10k.htm), fetched 2026-10-01). The company is now LegalShield; the structure may have changed since **(unverified)**.
- "Provider law firms are paid a monthly fixed fee on a capitated basis to render services to plan members residing within the state or province in which the provider law firm attorneys are licensed to practice." Capitated memberships were "more than 99% of our active Memberships". There is usually one firm per state: "a network of independent provider law firms, typically one firm per state or province".
- Discount: "Provider law firms have agreed to provide to members any legal services beyond those stipulated in the Membership at a fee discounted 25% from the provider law firm's customary and usual hourly rate." The average fee was "approximately $21" a month.
- Exclusivity: "Provider law firms are precluded from contracting with other prepaid legal service companies without our approval."
- Regulation: "We are regulated by or required to file with or obtain approval of State Insurance Departments, Secretaries of State, State Bar Associations and State Attorney General Offices depending on individual state opinions of regulatory responsibility for legal expense plans ... some states or provinces regulate legal expense plans as insurance." At the end of 2010: 33 jurisdictions had "No special regulatory classification", 13 had "Insurance company classification with required licensing for sales associates", and "40% [of memberships] were in jurisdictions that subject us or one of our subsidiaries to insurance or specialized legal expense plan regulation".
- Today: personal plan "$35.95 +/mo" billed annually. More complex matters get "a discount off their standard hourly rates". The site says the membership "is not insurance" ([legalshield.com](https://www.legalshield.com/), fetched 2026-10-01).

**The point for Duly: in a plan, money flows from the plan to the lawyers.** Students pay Duly. Duly pays panel firms a fixed amount per member or per consult, and firms give members a discount on extra work. Duly's margin is subscription revenue less what it pays firms. Nothing is taken from a lawyer's fee. New Jersey accepted that LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer did this, and their only fault was not registering (section 2).

### 5.2 Where plans are regulated as insurance (examples)

- **Florida, ch. 642 (Legal Expense Insurance):** "'Legal expense insurance' means a contractual obligation to provide specific legal services, or to reimburse for specific legal expenses, in consideration of a specified payment for an interval of time" ([Fla. Stat. 642.015](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/642.015), fetched 2026-10-01). A Duly subscription that promises a set number of attorney consults would fit that definition. Licensing details under 642.011 to 642.049 were not read **(unverified)**.
- **California Insurance Code 119.6:** "Legal insurance includes the assumption of a contractual obligation to reimburse the insured against all or a portion of his fees, costs, and expenses related to or arising out of services performed by ... an attorney". It excludes "(b) Plans providing no benefits other than consultation and advice on matters in connection with, or a part of, referral services" ([Cal. Ins. Code 119.6](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&sectionNum=119.6), fetched 2026-10-01). BPC 6155(c)(2) excludes from the referral-service law "A group or prepaid legal plan ... which ... (A) ... recommends, furnishes, or pays for legal services to its members or beneficiaries [and] (B) ... provides telephone advice or personal consultation."
- **Registration and filing:** New Jersey (AOC registration, RPC 7.3(e)(4)(vii)) and New York (annual report, Rule 7.2(b)(4)(vi)), section 4.

### 5.3 What a plan would mean for Duly's own rules

- It reverses market.md's line "No bundled attorney consult (BOUNDARY)" and VALUE-2 "Still no". It also changes the price ladder: a plan tier would sit above $100 a year, like TrackMyOPT Dedicated with its "one complimentary 60-minute initial attorney consultation" (market.md).
- Plan lawyers must be independent of Duly. For a for-profit sponsor, NJ requires that they not be "employed, directed, supervised or selected by it". Florida and Illinois forbid steering the lawyer's work, so Duly could not script what plan lawyers say. That still fits BOUNDARY, because Duly itself concludes nothing.
- Duly bears utilization risk unless firms are capitated.

## 6. Lawyer referral services

- **What they are:** a service that refers a person to a lawyer (it may choose the lawyer), in return for a fee, usually from the lawyer. Under the Model Rule, a lawyer may pay one only if it is "qualified", meaning approved by a state authority. The services may be for profit in some states (California certified services and Florida "approved" services under chapter 8) **(details unverified)**. In New Jersey and New York they must be run, sponsored or approved by a bar association (or authorized by law).
- **Percentage fees** are common at bar-run services and are expressly allowed in Illinois for "bar association or legal aid organization" services ("may include any fees or charges that are calculated ... based upon the lawyer's anticipated or actual attorney's fees", Illinois comment [7]) and in Florida for the Florida Bar service or approved services (4-7.22(d)(2)).
- **Why it does not fit Duly (estimate):**
  - It needs a certificate in each state that has one: California 6155, Texas 952, plus others not checked here.
  - It is limited to bar or nonprofit operators in New York and New Jersey.
  - It comes with caps and service duties: Texas charges a client no more than $20 for the first 30 minutes; California requires programs for "persons of limited means" (6155(e)(5)) and makes member lawyers carry E&O insurance.
  - Duly would then be choosing lawyers for students, which sits badly with BOUNDARY.
- **Partner instead of become:** Duly can point students to bar-run services (state and local bar lawyer referral services, and the AILA search already in BOUNDARY), with no payment either way. That is the current BOUNDARY design.

## 7. The founder's three models, one by one

### 7.1 A flat firm subscription ("law firms pay to stay on Duly")

**Allowed, as advertising, in all six states, if built this way:**
1. **Same price for every firm** for a fixed period, not tied to leads, consults, retained clients or fees. Tiers by firm size or by number of states listed are fine. Tiers by results are not **(estimate, from the advertising-cost reasoning in section 2)**.
2. **The student searches and chooses.** Filters are things the student picks: state, language, price, services offered, available slots. The order is neutral, for example random rotation or alphabetical. **No "recommended", "best match", "top rated by Duly", "Duly-vetted" or guarantee.** Objective checks are fine if worded as facts ("Licensed in good standing in New York as of 2026-10-01, checked on the state bar site"). Illinois requires published criteria if Duly rates lawyers at all (7.2(c)(7)).
3. **Duly does not use the student's case to choose the lawyer.** This is the NC comment [5] line. A student may share the case with a lawyer they have chosen, through the Share flow, with consent (B10 design).
4. **Labels.** "Paid listing" on every listing. "Firms pay Duly a flat fee to appear here. Duly does not recommend lawyers and does not get any part of their fees." The second sentence is required in spirit by NC comment [5] and Illinois 7.2(c). It also covers the FTC material-connection rule (section 9).
5. **California:** a signed joint advertising agreement with every listed firm, "under which the attorney or law firm expressly takes liability for the content of the advertising" (BPC 6155(g)(1)(B)). Show the firm names so the student "may select and initiate contact with" them.
6. **Florida:** meet all 12 conditions of 4-7.22(d). Report participating Florida lawyers to the Bar each year, show office location, have at least 4 firms, never imply Duly is a law firm, and answer bar counsel within 15 days. Florida-licensed lawyers must report joining within 15 days.
7. **Illinois:** give each Illinois lawyer the ICS certification letter. Illinois lawyers must disclose the relationship and any fee to the client.
8. **Texas:** keep it plainly joint advertising "intended solely to solicit clients for those lawyers" (Occ. Code 952.003(4)). Do not match.
9. **"Stay on Duly":** firms may be removed for objective reasons (licence lapse, discipline, complaints process). Removal must not depend on how many students hired them. Do not require exclusivity or bar firms from serving students outside Duly (Illinois comment [8A]).

**Risks:**
- A student in a frightening moment reads any listing inside Duly as an endorsement (channels.md 4.2). Ethics regulators judge "recommendation" by the reasonable impression created (NY 1132).
- Lawyers in states not checked here may face stricter rules **(unverified)**.
- Listing a firm whose lawyer later harms a student brings reputational harm and possible negligent-selection claims **(unverified as a legal theory; counsel)**.
- Revenue is modest: $36k to $300k a year at 30 to 100 firms (channels.md 4.3, **estimate**).

### 7.2 A percentage cut of the fee

**Not allowed for Duly with independent firms.** Section 2 (all five Avvo opinions), Florida 4-7.22 comment, Illinois 7.2(c)(8)(ii), New York 7.2(a), Texas 5.04(a) and 7.03(e) with Penal Code 38.12, California 6155 and 6156.5. These rules bind:
- the lawyer, who faces discipline;
- in California, Texas and Florida, the platform too, through civil penalties, private suits and certification laws;
- in Texas, possibly the platform as a criminal matter (barratry).

**Routes that are sometimes suggested, and why they fail:**
- *"Charge the student a booking fee instead of taking it from the lawyer."* In California any entity operating "for the direct or indirect purpose ... of referring potential clients to attorneys" needs certification, whoever pays (6155(a)). In other states a student-paid fee per matched lawyer still looks like a referral service **(estimate)**.
- *"Call it a technology or payment-processing fee, scaled to the invoice."* NJ: "The label and timing of the fee does not transform it into an advertising cost." Illinois allows an ICS to process payments only if it "remits those funds to the lawyer at the time payment is collected" (7.2(c)(8)(iv)). A processing fee at the processor's actual cost is a separate question for counsel **(unverified)**.
- *"Take the cut only from Arizona-licensed lawyers."* Arizona removed ER 5.4 and proposed removing ER 7.2(b) (section 3.1). Whether an Arizona lawyer outside an ABS may pay a percentage to an unlicensed platform, and whether that lawyer's other licences forbid it, is **an open question for Arizona counsel**. A California lawyer cannot share fees with an out-of-state ABS (6156).
- *Illinois per-hire fee.* A flat "connecting fee for every connection that results in a potential client hiring the lawyer" is allowed **for Illinois matters** (comment [7]). Most F-1 work is federal and not an "Illinois" matter in the usual sense. Whether Illinois's rule covers it, and whether the lawyer's other states allow it, is **unverified**. It is not a percentage in any case.

**The only lawful way to earn from fees themselves is to own the firm (Arizona ABS, section 3.1).** The ABS's own lawyers then serve students at a student price, and Duly earns the firm's profit. This is a different business. See the risks in 3.1.

### 7.3 Discounted rates for students

**Allowed, with care:**
- **The firm sets and offers the price.** For example: "Flat $X for an OPT application review for Duly users", or "20% off our usual consult fee of $Y". Duly displays it. Duly must not set or cap a lawyer's fee (Illinois 7.2(c)(2) and [8A]). A legal service plan may negotiate a member discount, as LegalShield does at 25%, because that is the plan's bargain for its members.
- **The discount must be real.** FTC Guides: a reduction from "the actual, bona fide price at which the article was offered to the public on a regular basis for a reasonably substantial period of time" (16 CFR 233.1(a)). Advertisers must not "make a 'limited' offer which, in fact, is not limited" (16 CFR 233.5) ([eCFR 16 CFR part 233](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-233), fetched 2026-10-01). So each firm must state its regular price and keep evidence of it.
- **The lawyer must honour it.** California Rule 7.2 comment [1] calls it misleading to advertise a fee and charge more. Texas certified referral services: "A lawyer may not charge a potential client referred to the lawyer by a referral service an amount that exceeds the total cost the client would have been required to pay ... if a referral service had not referred the client" (Occ. Code 952.155(a)).
- **The discount must not be paid for by a cut.** A firm that gives a 20% discount and also pays Duly a share is the Avvo model.
- **Do not call it "discounted attorneys" in a way that implies Duly provides lawyers.** Florida 4-7.22(d)(11) forbids anything leading people to conclude the provider "is a law firm or directly provides legal services". BOUNDARY bars Duly from calling itself "lawyer" or "attorney". Wording such as "Student prices offered by independent immigration law firms" keeps the firm as the speaker **(estimate)**.

## 8. DSOs, universities and conflicts

These are risks from Duly's own context, mostly analysis rather than black-letter law.
- **The school channel and the attorney network pull against each other.** channels.md section 3 sells schools the paid extras for their students and promises the school "never sees a student's record". If Duly also earns from firms listed inside the same app, a school is paying for a tool that shows its students paid lawyer ads. International offices may see that as a vendor conflict, or as the school appearing to endorse private lawyers. Many universities offer free student legal services, sometimes including immigration **(unverified for any named school)**, and may not want students paying "discounted" rates for what the school gives free. **Proposal:** a school-licensed Duly hides the paid directory by default, or the contract states plainly that it is there and lets the school switch it off. Show free options first (the school's own student legal services where they exist, bar-run referral services, and the AILA search).
- **Never pay a DSO, an international office or school staff for sending students to Duly's listed firms, or to Duly in connection with them.** Under 7.2(b), payments to anyone for recommending a lawyer are barred. California's runner and capper statute (BPC 6152, amended by SB 37) and Texas barratry (Penal Code 38.12(a)(4)) reach the payer. Public universities also have state gift and ethics rules for employees **(unverified, varies by state)**.
- **DSOs are not attorneys, and Duly should not position them as gatekeepers to paid lawyers.** BOUNDARY's step back sends the student to the DSO for school-controlled questions and to an attorney for matters beyond the DSO. Keep the DSO question and the attorney directory separate on screen, so a DSO is never shown as having approved a listed lawyer. channels.md 3.4 already notes "DSOs are careful about endorsing outside advice".
- **Student data.** Nothing from the case goes to a firm without the student's explicit share (B10). A firm subscription must never buy access to student data, leads lists or "students like you" signals. That would be selling leads built from immigration-status data, which is a privacy and trust failure even where the bar rules might allow paid leads **(estimate)**.

## 9. Consumer protection (FTC and state advertising law)

- **FTC Act section 5** forbids deceptive acts or practices. The DoNotPay order (final 2025-02-11) bars that company from "advertising that its service performs like a real lawyer unless it has sufficient evidence to back it up". The FTC noted it "did not hire or retain attorneys to test the quality and accuracy of its service's law-related features" ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires), fetched 2026-10-01). Claims about attorney quality inside Duly need the same evidence standard.
- **Price claims:** 16 CFR 233.1 and 233.5, quoted in 7.3. "Save up to 50% on immigration attorneys" needs a real comparison basis, firm by firm.
- **Paid placement and endorsements.** 16 CFR 255.5: "When there exists a connection between the endorser and the seller of the advertised product that might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and that connection is not reasonably expected by the audience, such connection must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously" ([eCFR 16 CFR 255.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-255.5), fetched 2026-10-01). A trusted compliance app showing lawyers is exactly where users do not expect a paid connection. Disclose it on every listing.
- **Reviews:** if Duly shows student reviews of firms, the FTC's 2024 rule on fake reviews and testimonials applies (16 CFR part 465) **(not fetched in this pass, unverified detail)**. NY 1132 counted Avvo's ratings as part of the vouching.
- **State lawyer-advertising laws reach the platform.** California BPC 6157 and following, as amended by SB 37, now covers communications by "individuals or entities that participate in joint legal advertising arrangements". A consumer who was misled may complain to the State Bar (SB 37 digest). Florida 4-7.22(d)(1) requires the provider to make no communication that would break the lawyer rules.
- **Notario and immigration-consultant laws.** Many states (California, Illinois, New York, Texas and others) regulate non-lawyers who offer immigration help **(not fetched in this pass, unverified)**. A Duly directory that blurs "Duly" with "attorney" raises that exposure as well. BOUNDARY's banned-words list already covers Duly's own voice.
