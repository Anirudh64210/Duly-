# Product round 1: resolutions of the engine builder's escalation

The engine builder built sections 1 to 6 and stopped on 66 tests that contradict SPEC.md or the output contract (engine/PROGRESS.md, run l-engine-b63b2c6ea2, "Disputes"). The builder was right on every one. This file settles them. Where it and a test disagree, this file and SPEC.md win.

## Settled

1. A question's id may hold dots. The contract says so now.
2. An answer dated after the day the case is run is refused. A test that dates an answer later than its case's day is wrong: date the answer on or before the day of the case.
3. `no_employment_to_record` covers the days through the day it was given. When the case runs on a later day, the question is open again and the count is null until it is answered again.
4. While a conflict of SPEC section 1 is open, the items that use its dates are null. An older test that expects a date or a count from a case that holds such a conflict is wrong: either the case is changed so that its facts fit, or the test expects null and the conflict.
5. An item whose step is recorded as done is not an open item (SPEC section 4). An older test that expects it as open is wrong.
6. A question carries `id`, `because` and `about`.
7. New: an answer with no `about` is not an answer. The question stays open. Duly never assumes that an answer was given for the values in front of it.
8. New: `parse_case(raw, clock)` takes the clock, so that an answer dated after today is refused however the engine is called. The api passes its clock and turns the ValueError into a 422 in plain words.
9. `unemployment.days_used` on a STEM card names both limit rules, because the days of the OPT card are in the count.
10. A card whose last day is the day its months end is not a conflict.
11. New: a recorded filing is checked against the window the regulation gives it. The rules are already in the pack, verbatim and settled: `opt.file_within_days_of_recommendation` (8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(B)(2): "The student must also file his or her Form I-765 or successor form with USCIS within 30 days of the date the DSO enters the recommendation for OPT into his or her SEVIS record.") and `stem.file_within_days_of_recommendation` (8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(C): "The student seeking such 24-month OPT extension must properly file his or her Form I-765 or successor form with USCIS within 60 days of the date the DSO enters the recommendation for the OPT extension into his or her SEVIS record."). The words and citations are the pack's, from its saved snapshot of 2026-09-14. Four conflicts join the table of SPEC section 1 and work as every conflict there does: the verdict is `cannot_tell` with `conflict:<id>`, the question asks whether these are the dates on the person's documents, a yes makes the verdict `talk_to_dso` with the judgement item `<id>`, and Duly says nothing about what the dates mean. It never says a filing was late.

| id | The facts do not fit when |
|---|---|
| opt.i765_filed_date.before_recommendation | The OPT filing is dated before the OPT recommendation |
| stem.i765_filed_date.before_recommendation | The STEM filing is dated before the STEM recommendation |
| opt.i765_filed_date.after_file_by | The OPT filing is dated after the recommendation plus `opt.file_within_days_of_recommendation` days |
| stem.i765_filed_date.after_file_by | The STEM filing is dated after the recommendation plus `stem.file_within_days_of_recommendation` days |

Each names its rule in `rule_ids`. The day count is the engine's, from the rule's value; no number is written in code. A filing on the recommendation day, or on the last day of the window, fits. When either date is missing, none of the four is raised. Why: a record that says a filing came before the recommendation it needs, or after its window, is either a typing slip or a matter for the DSO, and a quiet verdict on it is the failure PRINCIPLES.md ranks worst.

12. New: `next.grace.ends` is removed from SPEC section 6. Its formula is the formula of `grace.ends`, which `upcoming` already holds whenever it lies after today, so the screen listed one date twice (review of product round 1, note 3). `upcoming` holds each dated item once.
13. New: the screen names each legal status code in plain words from one fixed list beside the other labels, and the code itself always comes from the pack and the output (review of product round 1, note 2). The list names what the code is and nothing more: `enjoined` is "blocked by a court". SPEC section 7's "the words come from the pack" means the code; a word the pack does not carry is never shown as the law's own. For the founder: if the plain words should live in the pack instead, that is a contract change, and it lands first and alone.

The resolutions below come from a sweep for wrong or falsely reassuring answers (2026-09-29). Each was reproduced through the engine with the shipped pack, each quote was found in the saved snapshot, and each was checked against BOUNDARY.md, PRINCIPLES.md and the decisions. The cases are named with each point.

14. New conflict `stem.i765_filed_date.before_window_opens`: the STEM filing is dated before `opt.ead_end` minus `stem.apply_before_expiry_days` (8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(C): "up to 90 days prior to the expiration date of the student's current OPT employment authorization"). It works as every conflict of section 1; `about` holds `opt.ead_end` and `stem.i765_filed_date`; not raised when either is missing. Case: OPT card to 2026-07-31, STEM filed 2026-01-10, 112 days before the window opens; the engine gave no_open_items.
15. New conflict `opt.i765_filed_date.after_window_closes`: the OPT filing is dated after `program_end_date` plus `opt.apply_after_days` of the operative regime (8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(B)(2): "no later than 60 days after his or her program end date"). The conflict uses the operative value only; the enjoined value stays on the window items as their alternate (section 7). Only this side is checked: a filing before the window is not a conflict, because the case cannot tell pre-completion from post-completion OPT, and the one-year tolerance of the phase 2 SPEC stands.
16. New conflict `opt.ead_end.after_complete_by`: the OPT card ends after `opt.complete_by` (8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(A)(3): "A student must complete all practical training within a 14-month period following the completion of study"). Taking the card end as the end of training and the program end as the completion of study is Duly's reading, so the conflict carries `reading`. It is raised whether or not STEM is entered: the words except the 24-month extension, not the 12-month card. While it is open, the items that use `opt.ead_end` are null. Tests whose cases hold such a card change the case so its facts fit (point 4).
17. `grace.ends` is null while an entered OPT or STEM block has no `ead_end`, as every item whose input is missing is null, and as `next.grace.ends` already was. Case: STEM filed with no card yet; the engine gave a grace date counted from the OPT card alone.
18. `grace.ends` and cap-gap: while a cap-gap petition is active (as the engine already defines it) and `capgap.ends` is after the last card end, `grace.ends` is null, and the verdict carries the judgement item `grace_with_cap_gap` (talk_to_dso). Duly states no grace date then: 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(vi)(A) extends status and the employment authorization to `capgap.ends`, and what follows is for the DSO. Case: card to 2026-10-15, cap-gap to 2027-01-15; the engine showed a grace end of 2026-12-14, before the cap-gap it sat beside.
19. Unemployment on the OPT card stays held to its own limit after the STEM card begins. New item `unemployment.opt_card_days_used`: the days counted inside the OPT card, counted as `unemployment.days_used` counts, naming `opt.unemployment_limit_days` (8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(E): "Students may not accrue an aggregate of more than 90 days of unemployment during any post-completion OPT period"). When a STEM card is entered and that count is at or over the rule's value, the verdict carries the judgement item `unemployment_opt_card_at_or_over_limit` (talk_to_dso). The words say the count and the rule, nothing more. Case: 105 days on the OPT card, STEM running; the engine gave no_open_items with 45 days remaining.
20. A new Form I-983. For each employment period that begins on or after `stem.ead_start` and within the STEM card, the engine asks `employment.<n>.new_employer` ("Is this period with a new employer?"), with `about` holding the period's start and `stem.ead_start`. The words of 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(C)(7)(iv) set the condition: "If a student initiates a new practical training opportunity with a new employer during his or her 24-month OPT extension". Until it is answered the verdict is cannot_tell. Yes: the dated item `stem.new_plan_due.<n>`, the period's start plus `stem.new_plan_due_days`, an open item as any other. No: nothing. And for each employment period on the STEM card whose end is recorded before the card ends, the dated item `stem.concluding_evaluation_due.<n>`, the period's end plus `stem.evaluation_due_days` ("no later than 10 days following the conclusion of ... his or her practical training opportunity", 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(C)(9)(i)); taking the period's end as the conclusion of the opportunity is a reading and the item says so.
21. A STEM report date that has passed does not drop out. For each validation or evaluation item whose date is before today, the engine asks `<item id>.made` ("Did you submit this report?"), with `about` holding the item id and its date. Until it is answered the verdict is cannot_tell. Yes: the item is done. No: the verdict carries the judgement item `<item id>.not_made` (talk_to_dso); Duly says nothing about what it means. 8 CFR 214.2(f)(12)(ii): "Compliance with these reporting requirements is required to maintain F-1 status." Case: validation due 2026-09-28, today 2026-09-29; the verdict went from needs_you on the 28th to no_open_items on the 29th.
22. The end of an employment period. For each period whose end is recorded, the engine asks `employment.<n>.end_reported` ("Has the end of this period been reported to your DSO?"), with `about` holding the period's start and end. Until it is answered the verdict is cannot_tell. No: the open item `report_employer` for that period, dated the end plus `opt.report_employer_within_days`, with status guidance, shown as guidance. The regulation, 8 CFR 214.2(f)(12)(i), requires reporting "any change of name or address, or interruption of such employment" and states no number of days; the 10 days are SEVP's guidance, and the screen says so. The case's `employer_reported` flag records the employer, not the later end.
23. An item dated before the `effective_from` of another regime's row carries no alternate for that regime. The blocked rule of 91 FR 44976 took effect 2026-09-15 and says it "is not retroactive"; a filing window that closed in 2024 is never shown with what that rule would change.
24. Business days count Saturdays, Sundays and the days 5 U.S.C. 6103(a) lists, on those days. A holiday's observed day is not skipped: the saved words of 6103(b) apply it "For the purpose of statutes relating to pay and leave of employees", and skipping it made a report date one business day later than the cited words give, the risky side for a student. The date Duly shows is never later than the words it cites. This replaces "on their observed dates" of the phase 2 SPEC; tests that pin observed days change to the dates the words give. For the founder.
