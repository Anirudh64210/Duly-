   - a dark top bar with "Get Pro", a "Deals $8,136 free for you" chip marked HOT, and a light/dark toggle

   Going from the app to Help drops the user out of the app shell. That is the "hard to traverse" feeling.
2. **The sidebar has 15 entries at one level:** Home, OPT Dates, OPT Tools (expander), Case Status, Documents, H-1B Sponsors, Verified Jobs, Job Tracker, Career Tools (expander), Tax Filing, Health Insurance, Install Extension, Settings, Help, Chrome Job Prefill. Two of these are extension installs that do nearly the same thing ("Install Extension" and "Chrome Job Prefill"). Offers is not in the sidebar. You reach it only from the Deals chip in the top bar.
3. **Upsells are everywhere on every app screen:**
   - the promo strip: "Paste a job description. Get an ATS-ready resume in minutes: built for F-1 & OPT job search." with a [Build resume >] button
   - the "Get Pro" button in the top bar
   - the "Deals $8,136 free for you" chip marked HOT
   - "Get Pro" again under the user name at the bottom of the sidebar

   That makes four monetization prompts before any content.
4. **Immigration and tax statements sit in unsourced FAQ prose.** None of them cites a regulation, a USCIS or IRS page, or a date checked. The only outbound authority link is a generic "USCIS Official Site" button.
5. **The browser tab title on Offers reads "OPT Tracker & H-1B Finder fo…".** The value proposition is split across OPT tracking, H-1B sponsor search, resumes, jobs, deals and a Chrome prefill tool.

## Screen 46: Help Center, "About STEM OPT", "Tax Filing" and start of "Health Insurance"

**Screen and state.** `trackmyopt.com/help`, tab "OPT Help Center | F-1 Visa FA…". The page is scrolled partway down. All accordion items are shown expanded, with answers visible under each question and chevrons at the right. The content is a single centred column of about 680px with wide empty margins.

**Layout and navigation.** Marketing top nav as described above, with no in-page table of contents, no search box and no jump links. Each section has a small coloured icon and a heading.

**Content (questions and answers as shown):**
- **About STEM OPT**
  - "How do I know if my degree qualifies for STEM OPT?" The answer says the degree "must be on the STEM Designated Degree Program list maintained by DHS. Check with your DSO or search the official list." It names examples: Computer Science, Engineering, Mathematics, Physical Sciences.
  - "When should I apply for STEM OPT extension?" The answer: "You can apply up to 90 days before your current OPT expires. Your application must be received by USCIS before your OPT end date. Apply early to ensure timely processing."
  - "What if my STEM OPT is pending when my OPT expires?" The answer: "If you timely filed your STEM OPT extension, you can continue working for up to 180 days while the application is pending. Keep your receipt notice as proof of timely filing."
  - "What is the I-983 Training Plan?" The answer says it is completed with the employer, covers training goals and supervision, "must be submitted to your DSO."
- **Tax Filing for International Students**
  - "Do I need to file taxes as an F-1 student?" The answer: "Yes, all F-1 students who were present in the US during the tax year must file at least Form 8843 (even with no income). If you earned income, you'll also need to file Form 1040-NR. Filing correctly is a legal requirement."
  - "What tax form should I use as a non-resident?" The answer: "Most F-1 students should file Form 1040-NR… Do NOT use Form 1040 unless you meet the Substantial Presence Test. Using the wrong form can cause issues with future visa applications."
  - "When are taxes due?" The answer: "Federal taxes are due April 15. Form 8843 is also due April 15 (or June 15 if you had no US income). State tax deadlines vary. Use our Tax Filing page to see all important dates."
  - "Can I get a tax refund on OPT?" The answer: "Possibly! … F-1 students are also exempt from FICA taxes (Social Security & Medicare) during their first 5 years: if your employer incorrectly withheld these, you can request a refund."
- **Health Insurance on OPT** (start)
  - "Do I need health insurance on OPT?" The answer: "While not legally required, having health insurance is strongly recommended… A single ER visit can cost $5,000+… use our Insurance Finder."
  - "What insurance options do I have on OPT?" The answer lists four options: employer, ACA Marketplace (healthcare.gov), short-term, and international student plans, then says "Our Insurance Finder helps you compare these options."
  - "Does my school insurance cover me after graduation?" The question is cut off at the bottom.

**Friction.**
- Everything is expanded at once, so it reads as a wall of grey text. That defeats the point of an accordion.
- Light grey body text on white, at a small size, is hard to read.
- There is no search and no section jump, and the page is long.
- There is no link from an answer to the student's own dates. For example, "90 days before your current OPT expires" does not show *their* window.
- The answers keep sending users to other product pages ("Insurance Finder", "Tax Filing page") instead of answering in place.

**What works.** It covers the questions students actually ask, and the tone is plain. The STEM 180-day continuation and receipt-notice advice is practical. The 8843-even-with-no-income point is something many students miss.

**Accuracy and legal-risk concerns.**
- **STEM filing window is incomplete.** The page gives only the 90-day-before-expiry window. It omits the separate requirement to file within 60 days of the DSO entering the STEM recommendation in SEVIS. It also omits that the employer must be enrolled in E-Verify. A student can follow this answer and still file late.
- **Degree eligibility is oversimplified.** Eligibility turns on the degree's CIP code on the DHS list. A prior qualifying STEM degree from the last 10 years can also be used. Neither point is mentioned.
- **"Do NOT use Form 1040 unless you meet the Substantial Presence Test"** leaves out a key fact: F-1 students are "exempt individuals" whose days generally do not count for their first 5 calendar years. It also leaves out dual-status years.
- **"Using the wrong form can cause issues with future visa applications"** is an unsourced fear claim.
- **The FICA answer says "first 5 years".** The rule is keyed to calendar years and to nonresident-alien status. The wording could lead someone past year 5 to seek a refund wrongly.
- **"Form 8843 … June 15 if you had no US income"** is roughly right, since 8843 goes with the return or by June 15 when no return is required. But the page states it without a year or source.
- **"While not legally required"** is not true everywhere. Several states and DC have individual coverage mandates, and many schools require coverage. **Short-term health insurance** is listed as an option without warning that it may not meet those mandates.
- "A single ER visit can cost $5,000+" has no source.
- None of these answers shows a source, a jurisdiction or a "last reviewed" date.

  - "Does the extension work offline?" The answer: calculators work offline once loaded; "Case Status Tracker require an internet connection to check USCIS for updates."
  - "What does application Prefill change?" The answer is a paragraph of about 15 lines. It covers:
    - Prefill only fills empty supported fields.
    - Saved private answers are encrypted separately and not sent to AI.
    - "Saved portal login details, including password confirmation, fill when you click Prefill this application on a supported secure login or create-account page. No extra confirmation is required."
    - It "never clicks Login, Continue, Next, Create Account, or Submit".
    - Guided Autopilot pauses and stops before final submission.
    - "TrackMyOPT never submits an application."
  - "Why did Prefill leave a field or attachment blank?" The answer lists reasons: the field is already filled, there is no safe dropdown match, the private answer was not saved, the PDF upload was rejected. "Generated resume data expires after 30 minutes or when the job URL, company, or role changes."

**Friction.**
- A job-application autofill product's internals take up a large share of an immigration help page. This is the clearest sign that the product is "all over the place".
- The Prefill answer is dense and written like policy. A student looking for an OPT answer has to scroll past it.
- The free-tier description mixes quota units ("2 AI screening drafts", "1 AI cover letter", "100 shared AI writing actions") that are hard to compare.

**What works.** The page is candid about what the automation does not do: it never submits, never clicks Submit, and does not read inbox codes. The Pro and Free split is written down.

**Accuracy and legal-risk concerns.**
- **The extension stores job-portal passwords** and autofills them "No extra confirmation is required." That is a credential-handling surface. For a product that also holds immigration documents, it widens the trust and breach exposure a lot.
- The claim "Encrypted separately and are not guessed or sent to AI" cannot be verified from the UI.

## What this tells Duly (from screens 46 to 54 only)

- **Keep help inside the app, and personalise it.** TrackMyOPT's FAQ says "apply up to 90 days before your current OPT expires" in the abstract. Duly should show the student's own window from their own dates. It should include the 60-day DSO-recommendation window and the E-Verify prerequisite that TrackMyOPT omits, and cite the source and the date checked on each line.
- **Keep reminders free.** TrackMyOPT paywalls email reminders and document expiry reminders (screen 48). A deadline warning is a safety feature. Making it free is a clean, credible way to beat them on trust and price.
- **Keep deals out of the core product.** At most, offer a small, filtered "no SSN needed" resource list covering banking, insurance and tax. It should have per-item verified dates and a disclosure placed next to each paid referral. It should not have a dollar-total headline or urgency banners.
- **Keep the navigation to one shell and a handful of top-level items.** TrackMyOPT has 15 sidebar items, a separate marketing-nav Help page, and Offers that you reach only from a top-bar chip.
- **Show where every statement comes from.** Each fact carries a regulation or agency citation and an effective date, and anything that depends on the student's own facts says "check with your DSO". TrackMyOPT gives no sources, uses fear claims ("can cause issues with future visa applications") and overstates ("not legally required").
- **Do not ask for job-portal passwords.** Avoid the credential-storage exposure that TrackMyOPT's Prefill extension creates (screen 47). Keep the trust surface limited to immigration records.
