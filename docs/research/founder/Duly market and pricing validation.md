# Duly's market is real but the wedge is narrow

A real, monetizable market exists for Duly, with an honest verdict of "yes, but only if you sell the evidence layer, not the reminders." The pain is genuine and intensifying: roughly 90% of surveyed international students report fear about their visa status ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/nine-in-10-international-students-fear-for-us-visa-status/)), the spring 2025 wave of 1,840+ documented SEVIS terminations turned record-keeping into perceived survival behavior ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/global/international-students-us/2025/04/25/ice-reverses-course-sevis-terminations)), and the July 2026 duration-of-status final rule manufactures brand-new deadlines every F-1 student must now track ([Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-dhs-issues-final-rule-replacing-admission-for-duration-of-status-with-fixed-periods-of-stay-for-f-j-and-i-nonimmigrants.html)). The serviceable pool is 400-600K students per year inside a 1.18M-student population, concentrated in the ~294K on OPT ([IIE Open Doors 2025](https://www.iie.org/news/open-doors-2025-press-release/)). But the deadline-tracking layer Duly might be tempted to charge for is already free (DSOs, university checklists, DHS hubs) or nearly free (TrackMyOPT at $49.99/yr, VisaBuddy at $3.99/mo), while the unclaimed, monetizable territory is exactly Duly's vault-plus-WorkBook evidence layer: no competitor at any student price point offers versioned I-20 chains, automatic work-record keeping for RFE/audit defense, or cited personalized checklists ([TrackMyOPT](https://www.trackmyopt.com/); [Apple App Store, Lawfully](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lawfully-case-tracker/id1435063223)). The recommended model is a hybrid: a generous free tier for the calculator/tracker commodity, a Pro subscription at $79/yr (about $6.60/mo effective, sold annually), and one-time fear-moment packages at $29-$99, all web-billed, California-ARL compliant, and scoped carefully below the unauthorized-practice-of-law line. The dominant risks are policy (a proposed $100K OPT fee or outright OPT repeal would gut the market) and the fact that better personal records could not have prevented the 2025 terminations, only sped the response, so Duly must sell "ready in 24 hours," never "protected."

## The pain is real, and money already moves at fear moments

The evidence for chronic, recurring pain is overwhelming and institutional, not anecdotal. Universities do not build standing infrastructure around non-problems, yet ASU and CU Boulder maintain dedicated pages just to explain OPT unemployment-day math ([ASU ISSC](https://issc.asu.edu/news/calculating-your-opt-unemployment-days)), DHS runs an entire STEM OPT Hub because the I-983's 6/18-month validation and 12/24-month evaluation cadence confuses students at scale ([Study in the States](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/form-i-983-overview)), and at least five universities publish standing I-94 correction guides ([Chapman](https://www.chapman.edu/students/services/international-student-services/visa-status/i-94-corrections.aspx)). The emotional register matches: a Blind poster who forgot a SEVP Portal employer update asked "will I be deported? What are the odds?" despite holding active status ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/urgent-forgot-to-update-employment-in-sevp-for-opt-w06j6mm6)). That post also contains the pattern that defines Duly's real product: the pain is not "I can't find a form," it is "I don't know if I'm in trouble and have no authoritative, personalized answer."

Crucially, this cohort already pays, and pays in a bimodal pattern. Day to day they pay little: $4-$13/mo for trackers. At fear moments they pay a lot: **$250 attorney consults, $500-$1,500 for OPT/CPT compliance advice, $2,000-$4,000 for SEVIS reinstatement, $1,500-$5,000 for denial responses** ([Peter Chu Law](https://peterchu.com/blogs/medium-feed/f-1-attorney-fees-explained-what-youll-actually-pay)), and **$1,780 for premium processing that buys nothing but speed and reduced uncertainty** ([Immiva](https://immiva.com/blog/i-765-opt-filing-fee-2026-complete-cost-breakdown)). Premium processing is the purest proof of the fear-spending hypothesis: same outcome, four figures, purchased purely for certainty. More than 25% of surveyed students sought visa help outside campus resources, and only 38% had school-provided legal aid ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/nine-in-10-international-students-fear-for-us-visa-status/)), so the free institutional channel explicitly fails at the highest-pain moments; Berkeley's own guidance during the termination wave was "consult an immigration attorney as soon as possible" ([Berkeley International Office](https://internationaloffice.berkeley.edu/node/524)).

The honest counter-evidence: the routine layer is adequately solved free. Forum consensus on compliance slip-ups is "your DSO will fix it," and it usually does; DHS documents the unemployment counter itself, universities ship complete OPT walkthroughs, and both direct competitors lead with free-forever tiers, which is the founders of those companies telling you students will not pay for basic tracking ([SEVIS Help Hub](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-help-hub/student-records/fm-student-employment/unemployment-counter); [TrackMyOPT](https://www.trackmyopt.com/)). The unsolved, unpriced residue is personalization, crisis preparedness, and evidence assembly, which happen to be the things students currently buy from attorneys at $250+ a touch.

## Pillar scorecard: vault and WorkBook hit, digests and reminders miss

Mapping Duly's six pillars against evidenced pain produces a clear hierarchy. **The document vault with versioned I-20 chains and the WorkBook auto work-record are the strongest pillars**: attorney preservation guidance after the 2025 terminations (keep I-20s, EADs, OPT receipts, employer letters, paystubs, SEVP Portal screenshots, all ISS correspondence) matches Duly's vault nearly item for item ([Modern Law Group](https://lawofficeimmigration.com/blog/f1-sevis-termination-2026-due-process.html)), and MIT's standing advice to carry copies of all immigration documents converts the vault from convenience into daily hygiene ([MIT ISO](https://iso.mit.edu/news/new-advice-when-inside-the-u-s-please-carry-all-immigration-documents-travel-reminders-address-updates/)). No competitor offers this: TrackMyOPT's full feature list contains no evidence layer, Lawfully stores no documents at all, and VisaWatch deliberately narrows to case tracking ([TrackMyOPT](https://www.trackmyopt.com/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lawfully-case-tracker/id1435063223); [VisaWatch](https://www.visawatch.app/vs/lawfully)). "Evidence for STEM OPT reporting and RFE defense" is a genuinely unclaimed wedge: incumbents watch USCIS's state, none record the student's own compliance state.

**Timelines and deadline tracking match a loud pain but a crowded, free-served market**: this is the headline feature of both cheap competitors and dozens of free university and blog calculators, so it belongs in Duly's free tier as acquisition, not in the paid tier as value ([Sojourning Scholar](https://sojourningscholar.com/opt-calculator/); [TrackMyOPT](https://www.trackmyopt.com/)). **Cited personalized checklists are differentiated only by the personalization**: Harvard, Columbia TC and BU already ship exhaustive generic checklists free, so the paid version must be personalized across the student's specific I-20/CPT/OPT history, which conveniently also requires the vault ([Harvard HIO checklist](https://www.hio.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/USCIS%20OPT%20Application%20Checklist_02.2026.pdf)). **Impact-scored policy digests have demonstrated demand as engagement but weak standalone monetization**: NAFSA, VisaVerge and every university news page spun up free rolling updates during the 2025 chaos ([NAFSA](https://www.nafsa.org/blog/5-things-know-about-current-sevis-record-termination-situation)). **AI scenario answers are simultaneously the highest-desire and highest-risk pillar**: VisaBuddy already ships an AI assistant with regulatory citations at $3.99/mo ([VisaBuddy](https://www.visabuddyapp.com/)), and personalized "what are my options" output is squarely the language federal regulation uses to define unauthorized practice of immigration law (see the legal section). The pillar Duly does not have and should not pretend to: prevention. The 2025 terminations were government-initiated NCIC batch actions reversed by litigation, not by paperwork ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/global/international-students-us/2025/04/25/ice-reverses-course-sevis-terminations)). The defensible promise is response speed: a complete, versioned, timestamped evidence file in an attorney's hands within 24 hours.

## Price at $79/yr with $29-$99 fear-moment packages

The pricing evidence converges from four directions on the same band. Competitor anchors cap the tracker-shaped subscription at **$50-$155/yr** (TrackMyOPT Pro $49.99/yr, Dedicated $149.99/yr with a bundled 60-minute attorney consult; Lawfully Case Pro $154.99/yr) ([TrackMyOPT](https://www.trackmyopt.com/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lawfully-case-tracker/id1435063223)). Adjacent proven spend brackets the same range: Sprintax's ~$55-$105 per tax season is the de facto compliance bill this exact cohort already pays without complaint, OPT health insurance runs $41-$81/mo, and the identity-protection category (the closest psychological comp: protection you hope you never need) clusters at $108-$125/yr with 17-25% annual-prepay discounts and 60-day money-back guarantees ([Sprintax](https://www.sprintax.com/returns/); [ISO](https://www.isoa.org/opt_plans); [Security.org](https://www.security.org/identity-theft/lifelock-vs-aura/)). The cohort's mental ladder is clean: under ~$25 reads trivial, $50-$110 reads as normal annual compliance spend, $150-$300 signals a professional consult, $650+ is attorney territory ([USA Visa Law](https://www.usavisalaw.com/immigration-attorney-cost)). And the spending psychology is event-shaped: Gen Z shows severe subscription fatigue, with 8 in 10 subscribing for one specific need then cancelling ([CivicScience via NewscastStudio](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/01/28/gen-z-reports-high-subscription-fatigue-despite-maintaining-multiple-streaming-services/)), which maps directly onto immigration's discrete fear moments and argues for annual billing plus one-time packages rather than monthly subscriptions pitched in calm periods.

The concrete recommendation:

| Tier | Price | Contents | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free forever | $0 | Deadline timelines, unemployment-day counter, policy digest, basic vault (small doc cap) | Commodity layer; acquisition and SEO funnel, matching the market's free-tier norm |
| Duly Pro | **$79/yr** (anchor $7.99/mo, sell annual at ~17% off) | Full versioned vault + I-20 chains, WorkBook auto work-records, personalized cited checklists, AI scenario answers, export-to-attorney evidence file | Sits between Sprintax's annual bill and Aura, above TrackMyOPT's $49.99 because the evidence layer is unpriced territory, below the $150 consult threshold |
| Fear-moment packages | **$29-$99 one-time** | OPT Filing Pack ($49), RFE/Audit Evidence Pack ($99), Travel-Readiness Check ($29) | Anchored against $150-$300 consults and $650-$1,450 RFE responses; captures the subscribe-and-cancel cohort without churn damage |

Skip a $149 attorney-bundled tier at launch (TrackMyOPT already owns that shape) but treat it as the year-two upsell. Financially, $79/yr is 0.2% of the ~$38-40K/yr a student already spends to be in the US ([NAFSA](https://www.nafsa.org/about/about-nafsa/international-students-contributed-43-billion-us-economy-2024-2025-fall-2025)); the sale is stakes-framing, not affordability. The funnel math is sobering and should set expectations: **legal tech converts freemium at 5.6%, the best of any category**, versus edtech's worst-in-class 2.1%, so position Duly as legal/compliance, never as a student app ([First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-freemium-conversion-rates/)). Even so, 50K free users at 4-5% conversion and $60-$100 ARPU is only **$120K-$250K ARR**; freemium alone will not carry the business without the one-time packages layered on and, eventually, a B2B2C channel (the Sprintax school-code and Interstride institutional models are the proven zero-CAC routes to this audience ([Sprintax partners](https://www.sprintax.com/partners/higher-education/); [Interstride](https://www.interstride.com/career-centers/))). Lifecycle math caps consumer LTV: a STEM master's user is worth roughly 3.5-4 years (pre-OPT anxiety window + 12 months OPT + 24 months STEM OPT), non-STEM users churn after ~18 months, and with H-1B selection around 35% the dominant churn event is graduation out of F-1 status, not dissatisfaction, which argues for following users into H-1B tracking later ([DHS STEM OPT](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-help-hub/student-records/fm-student-employment/f-1-stem-optional-practical-training-opt); [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/h-1b-registration-numbers-for-fy-2026-h-1b-cap-reveal-an-increase-in-selection-rate/)). Opt-out trials convert at 49.1% versus freemium's 3.4-3.7%, so capturing a card during OPT filing season with a 14-day trial is the single highest-leverage conversion mechanic available ([First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-freemium-conversion-rates/)).

## Law shapes what you may charge for and how you must bill

Three legal regimes directly constrain the paid product. First, **unauthorized practice of immigration law**: federal regulation defines it to include selecting and preparing forms, advising how to answer form questions, and advising "what options a noncitizen might have," with most states criminalizing it ([CLINIC](https://www.cliniclegal.org/upil)). Duly's vault, timelines, record-keeping and general education are defensible self-help; the AI scenario feature is the exposed edge and must output general rule explanations plus "confirm with your DSO or an attorney," never individualized directives, and Duly must never auto-fill or select immigration forms. Boundless's three-layer disclaimer structure (informational only, no attorney-client relationship, explicit "you are not receiving legal advice" acknowledgment) is the template, and its legal services run through a separately licensed Arizona entity ([Boundless Terms](https://www.boundless.com/terms)). The FTC's DoNotPay order ($193K, prohibition on unsubstantiated AI-lawyer claims) makes marketing restraint a separate obligation: never claim the AI performs like a lawyer or is attorney-validated without real, documented attorney QA ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires)).

Second, **privacy**: immigration status is now statutory sensitive data under California's CCPA (AB 947) and the Texas TDPSA, which requires consent before processing it and voids its small-business exemption if sensitive data is ever sold ([Coblentz](https://www.coblentzlaw.com/news/citizenship-and-immigration-status-is-now-categorized-as-sensitive-personal-information-under-california-law/); [Termly](https://termly.io/resources/articles/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act/)). The 2025 DocketWise breach (116,666 immigration records including SSNs and deportation-defense details) is both the cautionary precedent and Duly's marketing contrast for an on-device vault ([ComplexDiscovery](https://complexdiscovery.com/when-your-legal-tech-vendor-gets-breached-docketwise-incident-exposes-116666-immigration-records-and-a-professions-blind-spot/)), and documented ICE purchasing of commercial data raises the stakes of holding anything server-side ([KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12055606/how-ice-is-using-your-data-and-what-you-can-do-about-it)). The trust claims must be scoped exactly: "documents never leave your device" is true for the vault, but AI questions transit the cloud, so no zero-knowledge claim may cover them, and "not retained by the AI provider" requires an actual zero-data-retention agreement before it is said ([Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)).

Third, **billing mechanics**: the federal Click-to-Cancel rule was vacated in July 2025, but ROSCA, roughly 30 state auto-renewal laws, and California's strict amended ARL (express unbundled consent, a direct online cancellation link, annual renewal reminders, pre-conversion notice on trials over 31 days) remain the practical compliance floor ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-06-04-california-automatic-renewal-law-amendments-take-effect-on-july-1-2025); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/05/ftc-revives-clicktocancel-rule-new-risks-for-subscription-businesses)). Sell on the web: after the Ninth Circuit's December 2025 Epic modification, even external payment links on iOS carry a commission, so web-first signup with mobile login is the only reliable way to keep the full margin ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/12/11/apple-app-store-fees-external-payment-links/)). Offer a 14-day money-back guarantee, matching Boundless's market norm and neutralizing the conscious-spender objection ([Boundless](https://www.boundless.com/satisfaction-guarantee)).

## Policy risk is the market's ceiling and its accelerant

The same policy chaos that sells Duly could also destroy its market, and the two scenarios must be planned separately. Near term (12-24 months), every policy move is a tailwind: the duration-of-status final rule (published July 17, 2026) imposes fixed 4-year admissions, cuts the grace period from 60 to 30 days, requires USCIS extension-of-stay filings, and starts unlawful presence accruing immediately at expiration, creating a whole class of hard deadlines that did not exist before and that map one-to-one onto Duly's tracking and evidence features ([Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-dhs-issues-final-rule-replacing-admission-for-duration-of-status-with-fixed-periods-of-stay-for-f-j-and-i-nonimmigrants.html)). Long term, the tail risks are existential: a **$100,000 OPT fee** was under White House consideration as of August 2026 with a DHS OPT rule slated for February 2027 ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/stuartanderson/2026/08/03/new-immigration-plan-considered-to-stop-students-from-working-in-us/)), and H.R. 2315 would eliminate OPT outright, though it shows no sign of passage ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/2315/text)). Meanwhile the funnel is shrinking behind the current stock: new enrollment fell 17% in fall 2025 ([NAFSA](https://www.nafsa.org/fall-2025-international-student-enrollment-snapshot-economic-impact)), so the OPT population likely peaks around 2026-2027 on the record 2023-24 intake and then declines 10-25%. The hedges are structural: keep burn low, cover the whole F-1 to H-1B lifecycle rather than OPT alone, and treat the university/B2B2C channel as the durable business if consumer TAM compresses. A secondary risk is trust: students frightened of data exposure may refuse to upload immigration documents to an unknown startup at all, which makes the on-device architecture and a public government-request policy first-order product requirements, not marketing polish.

Before building the paid backend, five validation experiments, ranked: (1) a fake-door pricing test on the existing free funnel, showing the $79/yr Pro tier and the $49 OPT Filing Pack with a real checkout page that ends in "join the waitlist," measuring click-to-checkout at each price; (2) 20 user interviews inside the Indian-student WhatsApp/Telegram cohort groups (the channel is large, organized, and publicly indexed ([WeMakeScholars](https://www.wemakescholars.com/blog/whatsapp-group-for-ms-in-us-fall))), specifically probing whether anyone has paid for OPT help, what they paid, and reaction to the evidence-file framing; (3) a concierge RFE/Evidence Pack sold manually at $99 to 10 students facing STEM OPT reporting or an RFE, testing whether the evidence layer alone commands money before any backend exists; (4) logged-in Reddit research on r/f1visa and r/USCIS for spreadsheet/tracking threads, closing the gap this research could not (Reddit was unreachable by tooling, and it is the densest first-person evidence source); and (5) three DSO/ISS conversations testing whether "record-keeping tool, not legal advice" positioning clears their endorsement bar, since Sprintax proved ISS distribution works when liability is low ([Columbia ISSO](https://isso.columbia.edu/content/filing-your-non-resident-tax-forms-using-sprintax-f-and-j)). One warning from the evidence: no first-person account was found of a student winning or losing an RFE because of good or bad personal records; the causal value of record-keeping is asserted by attorneys, not yet demonstrated by users, and experiment (3) is how you find out whether the market believes it.

## Conclusion

The research reframes Duly from a tracker with extras into an evidence company with a free tracker attached. The money in this market has never been in reminders; it is released at discrete fear moments in amounts ($250 to $5,000) that dwarf any subscription, and the only durable software wedge is making those moments cheaper and faster by owning the student's compliance record before the moment arrives. That reframing also resolves the pricing question: charge like the protection category ($79/yr, annually billed, refundable), monetize the fear moments transactionally ($29-$99), and treat the free calculator layer as pure distribution, because legal-tech's 5.6% conversion only materializes for products that feel like legal protection rather than student utilities.

The deepest strategic insight is that Duly's demand and Duly's risk are the same variable. Enforcement chaos and rule changes create the anxiety that converts users, shrink the enrollment pipeline that supplies them, and could, in the OPT-fee tail scenario, delete the market entirely. A company built on that variable should validate with concierge revenue in weeks, not build backend for months, keep the option to pivot its evidence architecture toward the university channel or the H-1B population, and never promise prevention it cannot deliver. Sell the 24-hour evidence file, price it under one attorney consult, and let the policy environment do the marketing it is already doing for free.
