# Duly must compute, cite, and never advise

Duly can build everything it wants to build, but only in one specific shape: a deterministic rules engine computes every date and verdict, an LLM is permitted only to narrate what the engine computed, the user is the visible decision-maker at every branch, and the server is architected to hold so little that a subpoena or a breach yields almost nothing. The unauthorized-practice line is workable because federal regulation draws it precisely: "exercising professional judgment to provide legal advice" is practice, while arithmetic on the user's own dates against published rules and clerical organization of the user's own documents is not ([8 CFR 1001.1, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/1001.1)). Texas hands software an explicit statutory safe harbor purchased with one conspicuous sentence ([Tex. Gov't Code 81.101](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/government-code/gov-t-sect-81-101/)), while California polices the adjacent risks through privacy law (AB 947 makes immigration status sensitive personal information) and the FTC polices marketing (the DoNotPay order bans untested lawyer-equivalence claims, $193,000 and a claim prohibition) ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires)). Privacy compliance converges on a single build target: Texas-style unbundled opt-in consent plus Maryland-style strictly-necessary minimization plus an absolute no-sale rule satisfies effectively every state at once. The zero-error claim is achievable only for the deterministic slice, because even citator-backed professional legal AI hallucinates 17-33% of the time in independent testing ([Stanford RegLab](https://reglab.stanford.edu/publications/hallucination-free-assessing-the-reliability-of-leading-ai-legal-research-tools/)), which is the empirical argument for rules-as-code with effective-dated parameters, attorney-gated rule-pack diffs, and mechanical validators that reject any LLM output containing a date the engine did not produce. The security bar is set by the anti-case that is almost exactly Duly's category: DocketWise exposed 116,666 immigration records, including SSNs and attorney-client communications, through stolen credentials and a roughly seven-month disclosure delay ([ComplexDiscovery](https://complexdiscovery.com/when-your-legal-tech-vendor-gets-breached-docketwise-incident-exposes-116666-immigration-records-and-a-professions-blind-spot/)), while documented 2025-2026 ICE administrative subpoenas to tech companies make "the server cannot produce it" the only reliable protection for this user base ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/08/list-ice-subpoenas-tech-companies)). The net effect on strategy: Duly is a record-keeping and computation company whose AI is an interface, whose honesty is the brand, and whose backend plan is dictated, in a good way, by these constraints.

## One sentence buys Texas; product design buys California

The UPL analysis lands on a reassuring asymmetry: the disclaimer layer is cheap and the real compliance work is product design. Texas Government Code 81.101(c) excludes computer software from the practice of law outright, on the sole condition that the product "clearly and conspicuously state that the products are not a substitute for the advice of an attorney" ([FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/government-code/gov-t-sect-81-101/)), a carve-out enacted in 1999 after the Quicken Family Lawyer litigation ([Texas H.B. 1507 analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/76R/analysis/html/HB01507F.htm)). That sentence should appear verbatim as Duly's baseline disclaimer on every recommendation-shaped surface. California has no equivalent statute, but it also has no consumer-facing AI-chatbot UPL opinion; the 2024-2026 record shows the actual enforcement channel for consumer legal AI is federal deception law, where the FTC's DoNotPay order prohibits claiming a service performs like a real lawyer without substantiating tests ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires)).

The substantive line comes from immigration's own regulation. 8 CFR 1001.1(i) defines practice as "exercising professional judgment to provide legal advice," and 1001.1(k) blesses "preparation" only when it consists solely of filling blanks with applicant-provided information, for a nominal fee, without holding oneself out as immigration-qualified ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/1001.1)). Janson v. LegalZoom supplies the software translation: interactive document assembly can be UPL because legal judgment is embedded in the branching logic, and the court pointedly noted that the technological medium is immaterial ([Eric Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2011/08/missouri_federa_1.htm)). The design rule that falls out is this: **the more Duly's logic selects outcomes for a specific user (which form, which benefit, which answer), the more it looks like practicing law at scale; the user must remain the visible chooser at every branch.** North Carolina's post-LegalZoom settlement statute, N.C.G.S. 84-2.2, functions as a national checklist for what a legitimate self-help product looks like: show output before purchase, keep a licensed attorney reviewer on file for templates and content, publish a complaint process, and, notably, do not fully disclaim warranties or force hostile venue ([N.C.G.S. 84-2.2](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_84/GS_84-2.2.html)). NC's ban on warranty disclaimers conflicts with standard SaaS terms, so Duly must either carve NC users out of its limitation-of-liability clause or forgo that state's statutory harbor.

The consent architecture follows the incumbents' three-layer stack plus one Duly-specific layer. Layer one is signup clickwrap under the Meyer v. Uber standard: an unticked checkbox, a conspicuous hyperlink to terms adjacent to the action button, and unambiguous assent ([Justia, Meyer v. Uber](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/16-2750/16-2750-2017-08-17.html)), carrying LegalZoom-pattern language that Duly is not a law firm, forms no attorney-client relationship, and does not review answers for legal sufficiency, draw legal conclusions, or "apply the law to the facts of your particular situation" ([LegalZoom Terms of Use](https://www.legalzoom.com/legal/general-terms/terms-of-use)). Layer two is a dedicated interstitial before the first AI answer, restating the Texas sentence and requiring a tap, mirroring Boundless's in-flow all-caps disclosures for its non-attorney staff ([Boundless Service-Specific Terms](https://www.boundless.com/service-specific-terms)). Layer three is the set of affirmative acknowledgments incumbents collect: inputs are truthful and complete, no outcome is guaranteed, scope is limited (the Sprintax pattern, proven on this exact F-1 audience: "Sprintax is not providing legal or tax advice" and "The User is responsible for the accuracy" of inputs) ([Sprintax Terms](https://www.sprintax.com/terms-and-conditions.html)). Layer four is a short per-answer footer on every AI response, not needed for contract enforceability but serving the Texas "clearly and conspicuously" condition and rebutting any later claim that a specific answer read as individualized advice. Every acknowledgment gets logged with timestamp, screen version, and text version, because a regulator is not bound by the user's contract; the disclaimer stack manages the private-claim risk while product design manages the regulator risk.

Per feature, the rules are concrete. AI answers follow a fixed safe template: state the general rule with a citation, describe what people in a generic situation category typically consider, and end with routing to the DSO or a licensed attorney; the forbidden shapes are "you should file Form X," "you qualify for Y," and "answer question 7 with Z." Deadline math is safe as arithmetic on user-supplied dates framed as "based on the dates you entered, the published rule's window is...," never "you must file by," with computed dates labeled as dependent on input accuracy. The vault and checklists ship completeness-against-published-checklist indicators ("the USCIS page lists six items; you have uploaded four"), never sufficiency judgments, because "your evidence is sufficient" is precisely the legal-sufficiency review LegalZoom disclaims. DSO email drafts stay factual and user-edited, keeping the DSO as decision-maker. And Duly does not auto-select or auto-prepare government forms at launch: that is the Janson fact pattern, and Duly cannot even use the 1001.1(k) preparer exemption because a subscription fee is not nominal. When individualized help is eventually needed, the two proven structures are the Boundless independent-attorney model (limited-scope agreements, contractual independence) or owning a firm through an Arizona ABS as Rocket Lawyer did in September 2024 ([Rocket Lawyer newsroom](https://www.rocketlawyer.com/newsroom/rlps-approved-for-abs-license)); immigration's federal practice authorization means one partner firm can serve all 50 states, which makes the attorney tier unusually cheap to add later.

## Texas binds on day one; building to Maryland covers everyone

Duly's privacy exposure is broader than founders usually assume, because the usual small-startup escape hatches do not apply to this product. California's CCPA classifies "citizenship or immigration status" as sensitive personal information after AB 947, effective January 2024 ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2023/10/us-california-amends-privacy-law-to-tighten-regulation)), but CCPA only attaches above thresholds ($25M revenue, 100,000 CA consumers) Duly will not hit at launch. Texas is the opposite: the TDPSA has **no revenue or volume threshold at all**, lists citizenship and immigration status as sensitive data requiring opt-in consent, invalidates consent bundled into terms-of-service acceptance, and preserves the sensitive-data-sale consent rule even for SBA small businesses ([Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/07/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act-overview)). Texas is also the most aggressive enforcer in the country: the January 2025 Allstate/Arity suit, the first enforcement action under any state comprehensive privacy law, turned entirely on collection architecture (no notice, no consent for sensitive geolocation, SDK-mediated data flows through third-party apps), not on any breach ([Vinson & Elkins](https://www.velaw.com/insights/texas-ag-targets-allstate-in-first-enforcement-of-texas-data-privacy-and-security-act/)). The Arity lesson for Duly is blunt: third-party analytics and ad SDKs are where sensitive-data "sale" allegations originate, and for an app whose mere installation signals "likely non-citizen," even device identifiers are de facto sensitive. No third-party ad or analytics SDKs, period.

The strictest-common-denominator target is now well defined because roughly twenty Virginia-model states list immigration status as sensitive data requiring opt-in ([Recording Law comparison](https://www.recordinglaw.com/us-laws/data-privacy-laws/us-state-privacy-laws-comparison/)), Connecticut's SB 1295 (effective July 2026) pulls in any entity processing any Connecticut resident's sensitive data with no volume floor ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20260731-connecticut-amends-its-data-privacy-act-to-increase-data-protections)), and Maryland's MODPA sets the ceiling: sensitive-data collection limited to what is "strictly necessary" for the requested service, with consent unable to cure over-collection, and sale of sensitive data banned outright even with consent ([Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/privacy-and-data-security/now-in-effect-maryland-law-raises-bar-on-sensitive-data-data-minimization-and-children-s-privacy)). Building to MODPA plus TDPSA (opt-in, strictly-necessary minimization, absolute no-sale, a data protection assessment on file, whose reciprocity clauses make one document portable across TX/CT/OR/DE/NJ/MN/MD) satisfies every laxer state automatically and future-proofs against CCPA scale-up, where confining SPI use to service delivery and saying so in the privacy policy avoids the "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" link entirely ([Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/guides/website-disclaimers/limit-the-use-of-my-sensitive-personal-information/)).

The consent UX has a converged regulatory spec. The CPPA's 2024 dark-patterns advisory demands symmetry (the privacy-protective option cannot take more steps), plain language, and no manipulative pairings, judged by effect rather than intent ([Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2024/09/california-guidance-on-dark-patterns-and-privacy)); TDPSA independently requires a freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous affirmative act. Duly's implementation: a dedicated onboarding consent screen, separate from ToS, naming the sensitive categories in plain words, stating the specific purposes, with equal-weight Agree and Don't-agree buttons where declining still permits on-device-only features, re-presented for each new purpose, revocable with one tap, all events logged. Rights plumbing runs on the standard 45-day clock: in-app download-my-data, delete-my-account, and correction affordances, with login-based identity verification. Deletion with a client-encrypted vault means hard-deleting cloud rows within the SLA, purging backups on a disclosed rotation window, wiping the local vault and keys, and instructing the LLM processor per its DPA; crypto-shredding of any server-held ciphertext is the standard pattern, though no regulator has formally blessed it, which belongs on the counsel list.

Two claim-scoping rules govern everything Duly says publicly. First, the marketing claim must be two-part and literal: documents and their contents stay on your device; questions you ask the assistant and your account and checklist data are processed on our servers and by our AI provider under contract. An unqualified "your data never leaves your device" while running cloud AI queries is textbook FTC Section 5 deception, and the FTC's September 2025 6(b) orders show consumer AI apps' data-handling representations are under direct scrutiny ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions)). Second, the government-request policy is a launch artifact, not an afterthought: publish which data categories exist server-side, state that vault contents are client-side encrypted and unproducible as plaintext, that requests require valid legal process and will be reviewed, narrowed, and challenged, and that users are notified unless legally barred. One adjacent flag: California's SB 243 companion-chatbot law almost certainly excludes Duly's task-bound assistant, but only if the product avoids a persistent relationship-sustaining persona ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law)); the "immigration buddy" framing should stay marketing-internal, and the shipped assistant should remain session-scoped and tool-like.

## The engine computes everything; the LLM may only rephrase it

The "recommendations literally cannot break" requirement has a known architecture, proven in tax and benefits: rules as code. OpenFisca, the pattern's reference implementation, stores every legal quantity as a versioned parameter whose values are keyed by effective dates, with per-value legal citations and an `expected` flag for announced-but-not-yet-effective changes, so "what was the rule on date X" is a lookup rather than a code change ([OpenFisca docs](https://openfisca.org/doc/coding-the-legislation/legislation_parameters.html)). Catala, built with French tax and benefits administrations, adds the strongest assurance form (lawyer-programmer pairing on formalization, formally verified compilation) and its formalization process famously uncovered a bug in an official government implementation ([Catala paper, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03198)). PolicyEngine contributes the QA pattern: versioned validation documents per release and differential testing against an independent oracle, its TAXSIM emulator ([policyengine-taxsim](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-taxsim)). No production immigration rules-as-code engine exists publicly; Duly would be first in the domain, which is both moat and risk.

The 2026 duration-of-status rule is the perfect stress test and the proof the architecture is mandatory rather than nice-to-have. DHS's final rule (published July 17, 2026, effective around September 15, 2026) replaces D/S with fixed admission periods up to four years, cuts the grace period from 60 to 30 days, requires USCIS extension filings, exempts cohorts with pending OPT applications, and makes untimely filers "begin accruing unlawful presence immediately" ([Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-dhs-issues-final-rule-replacing-admission-for-duration-of-status-with-fixed-periods-of-stay-for-f-j-and-i-nonimmigrants.html); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/17/2026-14439/establishing-a-fixed-time-period-of-admission-and-an-extension-of-stay-procedure-for-nonimmigrant)). The same student gets different answers before and after the effective date, with cohort carve-outs. A dated-parameter store answers this correctly by construction (`f1.grace_period_days`: 60 until 2026-09-14 citing 8 CFR, 30 from 2026-09-15 citing the Federal Register rule); a prompt-embedded-rules system cannot. And because unlawful presence triggers 3- and 10-year reentry bars, a wrong date here is not recoverable the way a tax penalty is; this is the one-way-door stakes profile that justifies the whole regime.

The LLM is banished from computation because the evidence says it must be. The Stanford RegLab preregistered study found even purpose-built legal RAG products (Lexis+ AI, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research) hallucinate 17-33% of the time, concluding flatly that "providers' claims are overstated" ([Stanford RegLab](https://reglab.stanford.edu/publications/hallucination-free-assessing-the-reliability-of-leading-ai-legal-research-tools/)), and dedicated temporal-reasoning benchmarks exist precisely because LLM date arithmetic is unreliable and input-shape-dependent ([Test of Time, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09170)). So the determinism boundary is drawn hard: the engine owns all date arithmetic, all eligibility verdicts, and all rule-version selection; the LLM owns only converting the engine's JSON into plain language, answering "what does this mean" grounded in retrieved rule text, and intake parsing that the user must then confirm. The boundary is enforced mechanically, not by prompt discipline: schema-constrained structured outputs ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)) plus a post-generation validator that extracts every date and number in the LLM's text and rejects the response if any value is absent from the engine's output set. When a situation falls outside the rule pack, the engine returns OUT_OF_SCOPE and the product shows a canned "we can't compute this, talk to your DSO or an attorney" card; the LLM is never asked to fill the gap.

Keeping the rule pack current is a solved pipeline problem at low volume. The Federal Register API is machine-readable and unauthenticated ([FR API docs](https://www.federalregister.gov/developers/documentation/api/v1)); the USCIS Policy Manual updates page and newsroom alerts catch sub-regulatory changes an FR-only pipeline would miss, such as the August 2026 policy expanding denial-without-RFE authority ([Jackson Lewis](https://www.globalimmigrationblog.com/2026/08/uscis-updates-policy-manual-expanding-authority-to-deny-incomplete-filings-without-first-issuing-rfes-or-noids/)), though USCIS pages 403 simple scrapers, so GovDelivery email subscriptions are the robust fallback; and CourtListener webhooks watch litigation, because the D/S rule will be litigated and an injunction must be able to flip a rule-pack value within hours ([CourtListener webhooks](https://www.courtlistener.com/help/api/webhooks/)). The filtered F-1/OPT stream runs a few items per week, not per day; law firms took days, not hours, to publish D/S alerts, setting the market bar. Realistic content-ops sizing is one founder on triage and encoding plus roughly 2-5 attorney-review hours per week steady-state, surging around major rules. Every rule-pack change ships as a git-style diff (parameter, old value, new value, effective date, citation, source snapshot) that the attorney approves as a unit, and the `expected`-value pattern means a July-announced, September-effective rule is encoded, reviewed, and tested weeks before it activates.

The QA regime is a stack, each layer covering a distinct failure class: a golden-case regression suite pinning every cohort and edge (filed day 89 versus 91, leap years, each D/S transition cohort); property-based tests asserting invariants (grace period end after program end, unemployment days never negative, recomputation under rule-pack vN-1 reproduces archived answers byte-for-byte); a differential oracle re-implementing the date math independently and diffing in CI; promptfoo-style CI evals on the narration layer asserting that all output values are a subset of engine output, every legal claim carries a resolvable citation, and out-of-scope probes get refusals; canary rollout of rule packs with staged replay of the golden suite plus anonymized real timelines, attorney review of any changed answer, and instant rollback via immutable pack versions. Underneath everything sits an append-only audit log per answer: timestamp, case snapshot hash, rule-pack version, parameter values with citations, engine output, retrieval set, model and prompt, final text, and reviewer approvals. Human gating splits by blast radius: pre-publication attorney approval for anything touching many users at once (rule-pack diffs, broadcast "this affects your case" pushes and their targeting queries, injunction activations, new answer types, abstention copy), automated validators only for per-user renders from an already-approved pack. Moffatt v. Air Canada supplies the legal floor for all of it: a company is liable for what its chatbot tells consumers, and disclaimers did not save Air Canada from a wrong fact ([ABA Business Law Today](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2024-february/bc-tribunal-confirms-companies-remain-liable-information-provided-ai-chatbot/)). Correctness, not disclaimers, is the real control.

## Verification infrastructure, not model quality, is the moat over ChatGPT

The production legal AI record answers "why wouldn't a student just use ChatGPT" with an uncomfortable fact and a clear strategy. The uncomfortable fact: by October 2025, plain ChatGPT scored 80% on Vals' legal research benchmark, above the 71% lawyer baseline and within a point of the best specialized tool ([LawSites](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/10/vals-ais-latest-benchmark-finds-legal-and-general-ai-now-outperform-lawyers-in-legal-research-accuracy.html)). Raw answer quality is not a moat. What the surviving products actually sell is verification infrastructure: CoCounsel grounds only in Westlaw/Practical Law because "the web is noisy," runs KeyCite citation verification, executes 1,500+ nightly tests per skill, and enforces a scope rule, "we do not let CoCounsel do anything it has not been trained and tested on" ([Thomson Reuters](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/blog/why-your-legal-ai-needs-more-than-the-open-web-a-look-inside-cocounsels-guardrails/)). Harvey grounds in 500+ verified sources and frames human-in-loop as "the mechanism that makes AI-generated work defensible" ([Harvey](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/how-to-choose-best-legal-ai-platform)). EvenUp, the closest analog to Duly's consumer-stakes profile, pairs its model with 100+ expert reviewers and markets a quantified delta over raw GPT-4 (95% versus 80% on medical-charge extraction) ([EvenUp](https://www.evenuplaw.com/blog/leveraging-ai-human-review-in-demand-letters-the-evenup-difference/)). None of them trust parametric model knowledge for legal facts, and even so, independent testing punctured the strongest marketing: Lexis launched on "hallucination-free linked legal citations" ([LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.com/community/pressroom/b/news/posts/lexisnexis-launches-lexis-ai-a-generative-ai-solution-with-hallucination-free-linked-legal-citations)) and Stanford measured 17-33% hallucination anyway. Third-party benchmarks, not vendor claims, became the trust currency, and benchmark-dodging (LexisNexis and vLex each withdrew from a Vals round) now reads as a signal in itself.

The failure catalog defines what Duly must never do. DoNotPay marketed "the world's first robot lawyer" without ever testing lawyer-level performance and took a federal order for it. Wadsworth v. Walmart shows "we built a specialized internal tool" is no defense, since the fabricated citations came from the firm's own AI database ([GC AI tracker](https://gc.ai/blog/ai-hallucination-legal-cases)), and the fabricated-citation problem now runs at roughly 350-400 court decisions per quarter globally ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-lawyers-keep-citing-fake-cases-invented-by-ai/)). The transferable mechanisms for a consumer app on a personal case file are therefore: a closed, dated, versioned corpus of official sources (USCIS Policy Manual, Federal Register, 8 CFR, SEVP guidance) with a freshness check per source as the immigration analog of KeyCite's "still good law"; deterministic citation verification outside the LLM; a scope allowlist of named, tested skills with refusal beyond it; a claims-to-evals register so every marketing sentence maps to an internal eval Duly can produce on demand (the direct FTC lesson); and the audit log as product, since Duly's WorkBook is the audit log the user themselves benefits from. Duly holds one advantage no enterprise tool has: the personal case file. ChatGPT does not know the user's I-20 history, cannot version their documents, and cannot be held accountable; Duly's answer to "why not ChatGPT" is "our system is verifiable, current, private, and accountable about you specifically."

The unoccupied ground is the TurboTax guarantee. Intuit pays IRS and state penalties and interest caused by its calculation errors, never the underlying tax, for seven years, with an evidence-gated claim process ([TurboTax guarantees](https://turbotax.intuit.com/corp/guarantees/)). The structure works because it insures only the deterministic, auditable slice, and no consumer legal AI product currently offers anything like it. Once Duly's engine and audit log exist, a scoped guarantee ("if a deadline we computed from your documents was wrong under the rules in force, we pay X") becomes rational and is a consumer-legible trust artifact of a kind ISO certificates are not, with the honest caveat that no payout makes a student whole for lost status, which is exactly why the human-gating regime exists.

## Hold almost nothing, publish the proof, expect ICE to ask

The security playbook's positive cases share one move: architect so you structurally cannot produce sensitive data, then publish the proof. Signal's government-requests page shows the ceiling: the only data it can hand a subpoena is registration date and last-connection date, and it publishes every legal demand it receives ([Signal](https://signal.org/bigbrother/)). Proton publishes annual order counts including how many it contested (988 of 9,301 in 2025) and states plainly what it cannot decrypt ([Proton transparency report](https://proton.me/legal/transparency)); its 2021 French-activist episode teaches the sharper lesson that a provider can be compelled to start logging data it does not currently hold, so "we don't log X" is structurally weaker than "we architecturally cannot associate X" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/09/06/protonmail-logged-ip-address-of-french-activist-after-order-by-swiss-authorities)). 1Password's two-secret key derivation (account password plus a device-generated Secret Key that never leaves the device) makes a server breach yield undecryptable data, documented in a public white paper plus a dated audit list ([1Password white paper](https://agilebits.github.io/security-design/deepKeys.html)). Apple PCC models scoped cloud-AI claims, where every claim is backed by a verification mechanism rather than a promise ([Apple Security Blog](https://security.apple.com/blog/private-cloud-compute/)). The anti-cases failed on basics, not cryptography: DocketWise fell to stolen credentials with server-side plaintext-accessible immigration records and a disclosure delay that itself became the story; 23andMe fell to credential stuffing without MFA, and its bankruptcy turned the database into a saleable asset, drawing state AG warnings with no new breach at all ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/healthtechsecurity/news/366646117/23andMe-settles-multistate-data-breach-lawsuit-for-18M); [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/23andme-bankruptcy-sparks-data-privacy-concerns-should-it)). So: mandatory MFA or passkeys, breach-password screening, no standing credentials with bulk access, and an explicit privacy-policy statement of what happens to data in acquisition or bankruptcy.

The immigration-specific threat model is documented, not hypothetical. In 2025-2026 ICE served administrative (1509) subpoenas on Meta, Google, Reddit, and X, several targeting users who merely criticized ICE; the challenged ones were frequently withdrawn, while T-Mobile and PayPal/Venmo "disclosed data without apparent challenge" ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/08/list-ice-subpoenas-tech-companies)), and the government including ICE buys commercial data from brokers without warrants ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/25/nx-s1-5752369/ice-surveillance-data-brokers-congress-anthropic)). The ordered threat list for Duly: administrative subpoena for account data (challengeable, often withdrawn when challenged); credential-theft breach of the cloud store (the DocketWise vector); data-broker acquisition via SDKs (the Arity vector, eliminated by having no third-party SDKs); and compelled future logging (the Proton vector, mitigated by never building a server-side document upload path at all). The corresponding architecture is a three-zone model: documents on-device under OS-keychain-protected keys; cloud account data client-side encrypted 1Password-style, with an explicit recovery decision (a printed recovery-kit pattern is the defensible middle, since some Duly users may need documents in emergencies); and an ephemeral LLM zone receiving minimized, structured case parameters (visa class and dates, not raw documents), under no-training terms and, if attainable, zero-data-retention agreements ([Anthropic privacy center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996868-is-my-data-used-for-model-training); [OpenAI enterprise privacy](https://openai.com/enterprise-privacy/)). PII redaction is harm reduction, never a guarantee: benchmarked recall for Presidio-class redaction is 57-73% on messy text ([Grepture](https://grepture.com/blog/presidio-not-enough-pii-redaction)), so the claim "PII never reaches the LLM" is unavailable; structuring prompts so raw document text rarely leaves the device is the real control. Keep the server-side metadata schema so minimal that plaintext visa type, nationality, and A-numbers, the exact fields an enforcement subpoena would want, simply do not exist there, and skip phone-number collection (the Signal-username lesson).

The LLM and document attack surface is the newest and most Duly-shaped risk: indirect prompt injection, where instructions hidden in an uploaded PDF hijack the assistant, exfiltrate vault data, or make it emit dangerous immigration advice ("you may work without authorization"). OWASP LLM01:2025 and Microsoft's production stack converge on the layered defense Duly should copy: pass document text only inside spotlighted delimiters with an instruction hierarchy declaring document content data, never instructions; render output as sanitized text with no remote images or auto-followed links, killing the main exfiltration channel; require explicit user confirmation for any side-effecting action in a turn containing document content; isolate context per user; and run an adversarial CI suite of poisoned sample documents (hidden white text, instructions in PDF metadata) ([OWASP LLM01:2025](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/); [Microsoft MSRC](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/msrc/blog/2025/07/how-microsoft-defends-against-indirect-prompt-injection-attacks)). The communications posture is pre-committed, sized for a two-person team: security@ plus security.txt with a 3-business-day acknowledgment SLA, a vulnerability disclosure policy on day one (bounty later; a VDP is the baseline norm ([HackerOne](https://www.hackerone.com/blog/understanding-public-and-private-bug-bounties-and-vulnerability-disclosure-programs))), a public postmortem within 30 days of any user-affecting incident, an annual transparency report even when every line is zero, publication of any legal demand after counsel review unless gagged, and an explicit policy commitment to challenge administrative subpoenas rather than quietly comply, which EFF's 2026 coverage shows is now itself a reported fact. SOC 2 (roughly $15K-$50K for Type 2 ([SOC2auditors.org](https://soc2auditors.org/soc-2-audit-cost/))) waits until the university/B2B channel demands it; consumers never ask, and a published pen-test summary answers the journalist question more cheaply. Open-sourcing WorkBook gives a Signal-style "read the code that touches your documents" story, valid only if the open-source boundary matches the sensitive-data boundary.

## The Duly doctrine, its positioning consequences, and the backend it dictates

The five research streams collapse into one ordered rule set. Order matters: each rule is a precondition for the credibility of the ones after it.

| # | Non-negotiable rule | Source of force |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deterministic engine computes every date, deadline, and verdict; effective-dated, cited parameters; the LLM never calculates | Stanford 17-33%; 2026 D/S rule; Moffatt liability |
| 2 | LLM narrates only from engine output and retrieved cited text; mechanical validator rejects any value the engine did not produce; OUT_OF_SCOPE yields abstention, never best-effort text | RegLab; structured-outputs pattern |
| 3 | User is the visible decision-maker at every branch; Duly never selects forms, benefits, or answers for a specific person; no auto-form-prep at launch | 8 CFR 1001.1; Janson |
| 4 | Every answer cites official sources; every rule-pack diff, broadcast push, and new answer type is attorney-approved before activation; append-only audit log per answer | CoCounsel/NC 84-2.2 review norms; FTC substantiation |
| 5 | Four-layer disclaimer/consent stack (Meyer-grade clickwrap, pre-AI interstitial, affirmative acknowledgments, per-answer footer), Texas safe-harbor sentence verbatim, all acknowledgments logged | Tex. 81.101(c); Meyer v. Uber |
| 6 | Unbundled opt-in consent before any cloud processing of immigration data; strictly-necessary collection; absolute no-sale of any personal data; DPIA on file; no third-party analytics/ad SDKs ever | TDPSA; MODPA; Allstate/Arity |
| 7 | Three-zone data architecture: documents on-device only; client-side-encrypted cloud account data; minimized structured parameters to the LLM under no-training/ZDR terms; minimal plaintext server metadata | DocketWise; EFF ICE record; Proton logging precedent |
| 8 | Prompt-injection defense stack in CI (spotlighting, sanitized output, confirmation gates, poisoned-document tests); MFA/passkeys mandatory; breach-password screening | OWASP LLM01; 23andMe |
| 9 | Every public claim scoped to what is literally true and mapped to an internal eval; never "hallucination-free," never lawyer-equivalence, never unqualified "data never leaves your device" | FTC DoNotPay; Lexis/Stanford gap |
| 10 | Trust published before it is demanded: security page with data-flow diagram, law-enforcement policy committing to challenge administrative subpoenas, VDP, annual transparency report, bankruptcy/acquisition data commitment | Signal/Proton/1Password; EFF |

The positioning consequences are direct. **Record-first**: the doctrine makes the vault, WorkBook, and audit log the product's load-bearing core, which is exactly where the market research located the unpriced wedge; compliance and strategy point at the same center of gravity. **AI-as-interface**: the assistant is a narration and navigation layer over the case file and rules engine, marketed as a tool, session-scoped, without a persona, which simultaneously serves UPL positioning, SB 243 exclusion, and the anti-ChatGPT story ("verifiable, current, private, accountable about you"). **Honesty as brand**: in a category whose incumbents got caught overclaiming (Lexis) or federally ordered to stop (DoNotPay), scoped literal claims plus published evidence is not just compliance, it is the differentiation, and it is the only marketing posture the FTC record permits anyway.

Open items for counsel, none of which block architecture work: confirm the exact 11 CCR 7027(m) permitted-purpose list before finalizing the CCPA no-Limit-link position; verify whether crypto-shredding satisfies deletion in the states Duly targets; decide the NC posture (carve NC out of liability limits versus forgo its harbor, and whether NC State Bar registration is worth it); verify New Jersey's sensitive-data list includes immigration status verbatim; pull ABA Op. 512 and the California GenAI guidance full texts before any attorney-partner tooling ships; confirm Anthropic ZDR terms at trust.anthropic.com before the retention claim is published; assess warrant-canary enforceability (the research suggests skipping canaries for Signal-style publish-the-requests); validate the guarantee's insurance treatment before any TurboTax-style promise; and confirm the SBA size standard for Duly's NAICS code governing the TDPSA small-business exemption. Two genuine regulatory gaps to monitor rather than solve: no state bar has yet opined on consumer-facing AI legal chatbots, and no court has tested an AI immigration assistant for UPL on the merits, which means the FTC deception framework is the live enforcement channel and substantiation files are the live defense.

For the backend plan, the constraints resolve the big decisions in advance. The schema splits into the three zones before any table is designed, with immigration semantics kept out of cloud rows (opaque template IDs, not "F-1 STEM OPT pending" strings). The first services to build are the rule-pack store (dated parameters, citations, source snapshots, diff-based releases), the deterministic timeline engine with its golden and property-based test suites, and the append-only audit log, because rules 1-4 depend on them and the LLM layer is meaningless without them. The narration service is thin: structured-output calls, the no-new-facts validator, citation resolution, abstention handling. The ingestion pipeline (FR API poll, USCIS change detection, CourtListener webhooks, review queue) and the consent/rights plumbing (consent event log, 45-day SLA tracking, crypto-shredding deletion flows) round out v1. Sequenced this way, the compliance regime is not a tax on the backend; it is the backend.

## Conclusion

The most useful discovery across all five streams is that the constraints agree with each other and with the market. UPL law rewards the same design (user decides, software computes and cites) that engineering reliability demands (deterministic core, LLM narration) and that the security threat model requires (hold nothing the engine does not need) and that the market research identified as the wedge (the evidence layer, not the reminders). Duly does not face a tradeoff between compliance and product; the compliant shape is the differentiated shape. The second discovery is where the real moats sit: nobody has built an immigration rules-as-code engine, nobody offers a TurboTax-style guarantee on immigration deadline math, and no competitor at any price can honestly publish a Signal-style "here is everything a subpoena could get" page, because their architectures will not support the claim. Each is expensive to copy precisely because it is architecture, not features.

The forward implication is a sequencing discipline: the rule pack, engine, tests, and audit log come first and are the company; the AI, the disclaimers, and the trust pages are layers over them that are only as credible as the core beneath. The single largest residual risk is not legal or technical but operational: the attorney-in-the-loop review pipeline is the one component with no automation substitute, and the 2-5 hours per week it demands at steady state is the true minimum staffing floor of the business. Budget for it as infrastructure, because under this doctrine, it is.
