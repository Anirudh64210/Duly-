# Spending Behavior, Willingness to Pay, and Payment Psychology of US International Students (F-1/OPT/STEM OPT), 2024-2026

## Key Question 1: What do international students demonstrably pay for today around status/fear moments?

### Takeaway
Students already pay a well-documented ladder of status-related costs: ~$20-$105 for tax compliance software, $350 SEVIS fee, $470-$520 for the I-765, $150-$300 for attorney consults, $650-$1,450 for RFE responses, and $1,780 for premium processing purely to reduce waiting anxiety. The market has repeatedly validated that fear/deadline moments unlock spending far above normal student app budgets.

### Cited Findings
- I-765 (OPT/STEM OPT EAD) USCIS filing fee: $470 online, $520 paper (2025-2026); no separate biometrics fee since April 2024 rule change — [Immiva I-765 cost breakdown](https://immiva.com/blog/i-765-opt-filing-fee-2026-complete-cost-breakdown)
- Premium processing for I-765: $1,780 effective March 1, 2026 (up 5.72% from $1,685); combined with online filing, total ~$2,250 — [Immiva](https://immiva.com/blog/i-765-opt-filing-fee-2026-complete-cost-breakdown); fee increase also covered by [Vorys](https://www.vorys.com/publication-uscis-announces-increases-to-premium-processing-fees) and [BU ISSO](https://www.bu.edu/isso/2024/01/03/uscis-premium-processing-fee-increases)
- Ancillary OPT costs: university DSO service fee $100-$550, mailing/tracking $20-$45, optional attorney representation $250-$1,500; realistic DIY total ~$570-$1,020 — [Immiva](https://immiva.com/blog/i-765-opt-filing-fee-2026-complete-cost-breakdown)
- SEVIS I-901 fee for F-1 students: $350 (official ICE fee page) — [ICE I-901](https://www.ice.gov/sevis/i901)
- Sprintax Returns pricing (the tax product most universities hand F-1 students): federal from $54.95, state from $49.95, state-only $59.95, Form 8843 $19.95, ITIN application $19.95, FICA prep $19.95, amended returns from $79.95, post-filing support $19.95-$29.95 — [Sprintax Returns](https://www.sprintax.com/returns/)
- Immigration attorney consultations: "$300 for up to 1 hour, $200 for up to 30 minutes" (prepaid $250/$150); F-1 student matters flat fee ~$1,850; complex/forced RFE responses $650-$1,450 — [USA Visa Law fee schedule](https://www.usavisalaw.com/immigration-attorney-cost)
- OPT-specific attorney fee ranges also published at [Peter Chu Law](https://peterchu.com/blogs/medium-feed/opt-attorney-fees-explained-what-you-actually-pay) (not fetched in full; corroborates $250-$1,500 band cited by Immiva)
- International student health insurance: marketed "from $1 per day" (~$365+/yr) for compliance-grade plans, with university-mandated plans costing substantially more — [American Visitor Insurance](https://www.americanvisitorinsurance.com/international-student-insurance/students-medical-insurance-cost.asp); [WellAway cost guide](https://www.wellaway.com/en/blog/international-student-health-insurance-cost/)
- Universities publicized premium processing availability for OPT/STEM OPT as demand-driven, framing it explicitly as a way to resolve pending-application uncertainty — [MIT ISchO](https://ischo.mit.edu/news/uscis-premium-processing-expanded-include-f-1opt-and-stem-opt-applications); [Harvard HIO](https://www.hio.harvard.edu/premium-processing)

### Inferences
- The premium processing fee ($1,780 for roughly 30 days of certainty vs 2-4 months of anxiety) is the purest existing proof of the "students spend when in fear" hypothesis: it buys no different outcome, only speed and reduced uncertainty, and USCIS keeps raising its price.
- Sprintax's $20-$105 tier shows the compliance-software price band this cohort already accepts annually without complaint; it is the closest existing comp to Duly's product category (software that keeps you legal).
- The spend ladder is event-shaped, not continuous: money is released at discrete fear moments (OPT filing window, tax season, RFE, travel/visa renewal), which argues for pricing tied to moments/seasons rather than a flat always-on subscription.

### Gaps
- No public data found on what percentage of OPT applicants actually purchase premium processing (USCIS does not publish uptake by category).
- Exact university-sponsored health insurance premiums vary too widely by school to give a single authoritative figure; commonly cited $1,500-$3,000/yr figures were not verifiable from a primary source within this research pass.

## Key Question 2: Survey/study data on international student finances, price sensitivity, and subscription fatigue

### Takeaway
International students are high-spend by necessity (~$38-40k/yr average all-in) and US college students broadly are quality-driven rather than price-driven; but Gen Z shows severe, well-documented subscription fatigue, with a strong pattern of subscribing for a specific need and cancelling immediately after.

### Cited Findings
- International students contributed $42.9 billion to the US economy in 2024-2025 (first decline since COVID, -2%); fall 2025 new enrollment fell 17%, removing $1.1B in spending — [NAFSA](https://www.nafsa.org/about/about-nafsa/international-students-contributed-43-billion-us-economy-2024-2025-fall-2025)
- US college students overall: "college students spend $600 billion a year and account for 66% of all spending among 18-24-year-olds"; fewer than 50% named cost as their primary purchasing consideration in any category; 53% say quality matters more than cost for personal care — [flytedesk research](https://flytedesk.com/insights/are-college-students-cheap-new-research-on-student-buying-habits-and-price-sensitivity)
- Gen Z subscription fatigue (CivicScience data, Jan 2026): 37% cancelled at least one streaming service since December from feeling overwhelmed, 29% intended to cancel soon, only 13% report no fatigue; 8 in 10 signed up for a service to watch one specific show then cancelled/paused — [NewscastStudio/CivicScience](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/01/28/gen-z-reports-high-subscription-fatigue-despite-maintaining-multiple-streaming-services/)
- Despite fatigue, over half of Gen Z ad-tier users report spending over $100/month on subscriptions total — [NewscastStudio/CivicScience](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/01/28/gen-z-reports-high-subscription-fatigue-despite-maintaining-multiple-streaming-services/)
- Additional subscription-fatigue stat compilations exist at [TechRT](https://techrt.com/subscription-fatigue-statistics/) and [Chargeback](https://www.joinchargeback.com/blogs/subscription-spending-by-generation) (aggregators; not independently verified here)

### Inferences
- NAFSA's $42.9B across ~1.1M students implies roughly $38-40k/yr per student in tuition + living spend; a $50-$150/yr compliance product is 0.1-0.4% of their annual US spend, i.e., financially trivial relative to what is at stake (the entire $38-40k/yr investment plus future earnings).
- The "subscribe for one show, cancel after" pattern maps directly onto immigration: expect students to subscribe at an OPT/STEM/tax/travel moment and churn after unless the product creates continuous perceived risk coverage. Plan for high monthly churn or price annually.
- "Conscious spender" is the right refinement of the founder's hypothesis: this cohort is not cheap, it is ROI-driven; cost is secondary to demonstrated quality/stakes.

### Gaps
- No US-specific published survey of international students' discretionary app/subscription budgets was found (Save the Student is UK-only; NAFSA measures aggregate economic contribution, not app-level WTP). This is a genuine data hole; primary user interviews would be needed.
- No published data from Zolve/Adro/Juno quantifying international student spending patterns or ARPU was found in this pass.

## Key Question 3: Behavioral evidence, one-time vs subscription vs pay-per-outcome in fear-driven products

### Takeaway
Fear/insurance-adjacent consumer products (identity protection) overwhelmingly use low-double-digit monthly subscriptions with ~17-25% annual-prepay discounts and free-trial/money-back derisking; the $10-$15/mo band is the proven norm for "protection you hope you never need."

### Cited Findings
- Aura (identity protection): individual plan $12/mo or $108/yr (~25% annual discount), 14-day free trial, 60-day money-back guarantee — [Security.org Aura vs LifeLock](https://www.security.org/identity-theft/lifelock-vs-aura/)
- LifeLock: Core $12.49/mo ($124.99/yr), Advanced $19.99/mo ($199.99/yr), Ultimate Plus $34.99/mo ($349.99/yr); ~17% annual discount; 30-day free trial, 60-day money-back — [Security.org](https://www.security.org/identity-theft/lifelock-vs-aura/)
- Tax products in this niche are pay-per-outcome/per-event, not subscriptions: Sprintax charges per return/form ($19.95-$79.95 per item) — [Sprintax](https://www.sprintax.com/returns/)
- Attorneys price per event: consults $150-$300, flat fees per filing, RFE responses $650-$1,450 — [USA Visa Law](https://www.usavisalaw.com/immigration-attorney-cost)
- Opt-out (credit-card-required) free trials convert at 49.1% to paid but attract only 2.7% of visitors to sign up; freemium attracts 12.8-14.1% of visitors but converts only 3.4-3.7% — [First Page Sage 2026](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-freemium-conversion-rates/)

### Inferences
- The existing immigration-adjacent market is split by product type: recurring peace-of-mind products (insurance, identity protection) are $10-15/mo subscriptions; discrete compliance events (taxes, filings, RFEs) are one-time fees scaled to stakes ($20 to $1,800). Duly spans both, which supports a hybrid: a low subscription for monitoring/deadlines plus one-time "moment" packages (OPT filing pack, RFE panic pack, travel-readiness check) priced $29-$99.
- Money-back guarantees and free trials are table stakes in fear products; they neutralize the "conscious spender" objection because trying costs nothing.
- Pay-per-outcome at the fear moment likely converts better for this cohort than an always-on subscription pitched in calm periods, given the documented subscribe-and-cancel behavior.

### Gaps
- No public conversion-rate data specific to LifeLock/Aura (private figures) or to any immigration app (Boundless, Lawfully, etc. do not publish conversion metrics).
- No academic study found directly comparing one-time vs subscription framing in fear-driven purchases for students specifically; the Wharton subscription-programs paper ([SSRN id3529681](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/SSRN-id3529681.pdf)) covers general purchase-lift from subscriptions but was not fetched/verified in this pass.

## Key Question 4: Freemium conversion benchmarks to sanity-check revenue

### Takeaway
Plan on 3-5% free-to-paid conversion; legal-tech is the single best-converting freemium category (5.6%), which is favorable for Duly, while edtech is the worst (2.1%), so positioning as legal/compliance rather than "student app" materially changes the achievable rate.

### Cited Findings
- Freemium-to-paid by industry (2026, 80+ SaaS companies): Legal/LegalTech 5.6% (highest), Fintech 4.8%, EdTech 2.1% (lowest); visitor-to-freemium signup clusters at 11.6-15.1% across industries — [First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-freemium-conversion-rates/)
- Conversion improves where software handles "high-value business processes"; "once that value is demonstrated, paying for additional functionality may be easier to justify" — [First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-freemium-conversion-rates/)
- Opt-out free trials: 49.1% trial-to-paid vs freemium's 3.4-3.7% free-to-paid — [First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-freemium-conversion-rates/)
- Additional benchmark compilations: [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/saas-average-conversion-rate/) and [daydream](https://www.withdaydream.com/library/insights/freemium-conversion-rate) (aggregators, not fetched in full)

### Inferences
- Sanity math: if Duly reaches 50k free users (of ~1.1M international students; ~240k+ Indian students alone), 4-5% conversion at ~$60-100/yr yields roughly $120k-$250k ARR; freemium alone will not carry the business without either large top-of-funnel or higher-ARPU event purchases layered on.
- The 49% opt-out-trial conversion figure suggests capturing a card at a fear moment (e.g., during OPT filing season with a free trial) is far more lucrative than a permanent free tier, though it narrows the funnel.

### Gaps
- ProfitWell/Paddle and OpenView 2024-2026 consumer-specific freemium benchmarks were not retrieved in this pass; First Page Sage is the only fetched primary benchmark source (its methodology is its own client base, which skews B2B).

## Key Question 5: Payment logistics for this cohort

### Takeaway
An entire fintech category (Zolve, Adro, MPOWER, Firstcard) exists to give international students US-issued cards without SSN/credit history, and universities require US payment for SEVIS/USCIS fees anyway; assume US-issued debit/credit cards dominate and Indian RBI e-mandate friction is largely irrelevant for students already in the US.

### Cited Findings
- Zolve markets US bank accounts and credit cards with no SSN required, targeted at international students/workers pre-arrival and on arrival — [Zolve](https://zolve.com/us/credit-card)
- MPOWER Financing offers an international student credit card (no SSN, no cosigner) — [MPOWER](https://www.mpowerfinancing.com/international-student-credit-card)
- Multiple fintech alternatives to traditional banks are marketed specifically to this cohort (Zolve's own roundup of the category) — [Zolve blog](https://zolve.com/blog/alternatives-to-traditional-banking-fintech-options-for-international-students-in-the-usa/)
- Both major identity-protection incumbents derisk with 60-day money-back guarantees, setting refund expectations in the protection category — [Security.org](https://www.security.org/identity-theft/lifelock-vs-aura/)

### Inferences
- Since students already pay SEVIS ($350) and I-765 ($470) with US-usable cards, card acceptance is not a barrier; the real logistics question is willingness to store a card for recurring billing, which subscription-fatigue data suggests is the friction point, not the card itself.
- Annual-prepay with a visible discount (the 17-25% norm from LifeLock/Aura) fits both the cohort's academic-year mental accounting and the churn risk from fear-moment subscribers.

### Gaps
- No published data found on what share of F-1 students hold US-issued credit vs debit cards, or on their monthly vs annual billing preferences specifically; this was not directly measurable from public sources.
- No sources found on refund-request behavior of student app subscribers specifically.

## Key Question 6: Price-point anchors — what reads as trivial, considered, attorney-territory

### Takeaway
The cohort's existing spend ladder gives clean anchors: under ~$25 reads as trivial (Form 8843, ITIN add-ons), ~$50-$110 reads as considered-but-normal annual compliance spend (Sprintax federal+state), $150-$300 signals professional consultation, and $650+ is attorney territory reserved for genuine emergencies; $1,780 premium processing proves a four-figure panic ceiling exists.

### Cited Findings
- Trivial tier ($19.95-$29.95): Sprintax Form 8843, ITIN, FICA, post-filing add-ons — [Sprintax](https://www.sprintax.com/returns/)
- Considered/normal tier ($50-$110): Sprintax federal ($54.95) + state ($49.95) is the de facto annual compliance bill most F-1 students pay — [Sprintax](https://www.sprintax.com/returns/)
- Peace-of-mind subscription tier ($108-$150/yr): Aura $108/yr, LifeLock Core $124.99/yr — [Security.org](https://www.security.org/identity-theft/lifelock-vs-aura/)
- Professional-touch tier ($150-$300): immigration attorney 30-60 min consult — [USA Visa Law](https://www.usavisalaw.com/immigration-attorney-cost)
- Attorney-territory tier ($650-$1,850+): RFE responses $650-$1,450; F-1 matters flat ~$1,850 — [USA Visa Law](https://www.usavisalaw.com/immigration-attorney-cost)
- Panic ceiling ($1,780+): premium processing purchased solely for certainty/speed — [Immiva](https://immiva.com/blog/i-765-opt-filing-fee-2026-complete-cost-breakdown)
- University DSO fees of $100-$550 show students already tolerate low-hundreds administrative charges attached to OPT — [Immiva](https://immiva.com/blog/i-765-opt-filing-fee-2026-complete-cost-breakdown)

### Inferences
- Pricing guidance for Duly: a free tier for top-of-funnel; core subscription at ~$8-12/mo with annual at ~$79-99/yr (sits between Sprintax's annual bill and Aura, inside the proven protection band, and undercuts one attorney consult); one-time fear-moment packages at $29-$99 (OPT filing checklist/review, RFE triage, travel-readiness) that anchor against $150-$300 consults; anything over ~$150 will be mentally benchmarked against "just ask a lawyer."
- The hypothesis "students spend when in fear but are conscious spenders" is validated and refines to: spending is stake-proportional and event-triggered, not impulsive; they pay readily when a price is (a) clearly below the attorney alternative, (b) clearly protective of a $38-40k/yr investment, and (c) refundable or trialable.

### Gaps
- Direct Reddit/forum thread evidence (r/f1visa, r/USCIS discussions of paying for OPT help) could not be retrieved as fetchable primary sources in this pass; search results surfaced Substack and Blind aggregations ([example](https://aparnavashishtrota.substack.com/p/end-of-opt-what-immigration-attorneys), [Blind F1 search](https://www.teamblind.com/search/F1Visa)) but no quantified forum sentiment. Recommend manual forum review or user interviews to validate the $29-$99 moment-package band.
- No data found on premium processing purchase rates among OPT applicants, which would be the single best quantitative measure of fear-driven WTP.
