# Privacy-Compliance Requirements for Duly (US consumer immigration app, hybrid on-device/cloud), 2025-2026

Scope note: Duly processes immigration/visa status of F-1 students. Documents stay on-device; account data, checklist state, and AI queries touch the cloud. Focus: CA + TX, plus all states treating immigration status as sensitive, chatbot-disclosure laws, and build-ready consent/rights/engineering patterns.

## Key Question 1: CCPA/CPRA after AB 947 (California)

### Takeaway
AB 947 (effective Jan 1, 2024) added "citizenship or immigration status" to CCPA's sensitive personal information (SPI) categories, so essentially all of Duly's core data is SPI in California. But CCPA only applies above thresholds Duly will not meet at launch ($25M+ revenue, 100k+ CA consumers, or 50%+ revenue from selling/sharing data); below them, FTC Act §5 and honest-claims discipline still govern. If/when CCPA applies, Duly can avoid the "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" link by confining SPI use to service delivery and stating so in the privacy policy; GPC honoring is mandatory for opt-outs.

### Cited Findings
- AB 947 expands CCPA SPI to include personal information that "reveals a California resident's citizenship or immigration status"; examples include non-US passport scans, ITINs, and immigration-status communications — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2023/10/us-california-amends-privacy-law-to-tighten-regulation)
- Once classified SPI, the data is subject to CCPA's restrictions on processing SPI and the consumer right to request limits on SPI use/disclosure; businesses should update privacy policies to disclose collection of SPI that may reveal immigration status — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2023/10/us-california-amends-privacy-law-to-tighten-regulation)
- CCPA applicability thresholds (must meet at least one): annual gross revenue over $25 million; collects data on 100,000+ CA residents/households annually; or derives 50%+ of annual revenue from selling/sharing consumer data — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/guides/website-disclaimers/limit-the-use-of-my-sensitive-personal-information/)
- The "Limit the Use of My SPI" link is required only when SPI is used/disclosed "beyond those permitted purposes, such as cross-context advertising or profiling"; permitted purposes include delivering requested services/goods, fraud prevention and security, physical safety, legal compliance, and internal product-improvement research — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/guides/website-disclaimers/limit-the-use-of-my-sensitive-personal-information/)
- A business restricting SPI to permitted purposes is exempt from posting the link if it "state[s] this clearly in your privacy policy" — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/guides/website-disclaimers/limit-the-use-of-my-sensitive-personal-information/)
- GPC/universal opt-out signals must be honored automatically: a consumer exercising rights via GPC "must have their SPI restriction honored automatically," with no separate request needed — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/guides/website-disclaimers/limit-the-use-of-my-sensitive-personal-information/)
- Consolidated CCPA statute + regulations text (including notice-at-collection and §7027 SPI rules), current version effective Jan 1, 2026 — [CPPA statute/regs PDF](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_statute_eff_20260101.pdf)

### Inferences
- Duly's build target in CA: (1) notice at collection listing SPI categories (immigration status, government IDs) and purposes; (2) keep ALL SPI processing inside §7027(m)-style permitted purposes (deliver the service the student asked for, security, legal compliance) so no "Limit" link is needed; state that scoping explicitly in the privacy policy; (3) never use immigration data for advertising, profiling, or model training beyond the user's own session; (4) implement GPC handling on any web surface even pre-threshold, since it is cheap and future-proofs the stack.
- Below CCPA thresholds, the binding constraints are the FTC Act §5 (deception/unfairness over privacy promises) and the other state laws below, most of which have NO revenue floor for sensitive-data duties (Texas especially). Do not architect for "CCPA doesn't apply so nothing applies."

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the verbatim text of 11 CCR §7027(m) enumerated purposes or the CPI-adjusted 2026 revenue threshold figure; the CPPA consolidated PDF above contains both but was not parsed line-by-line. Counsel should confirm the exact §7027(m) purpose list before finalizing the privacy policy exemption language.

## Key Question 2: Texas TDPSA

### Takeaway
TDPSA is the state most likely to bind Duly on day one: it has NO revenue or volume thresholds, applies to anyone doing business in Texas or serving Texas residents, and explicitly lists "citizenship and immigration status" as sensitive data requiring opt-in consent. The SBA small-business exemption spares most duties but never the sensitive-data-sale consent rule, and selling sensitive data as a small business without consent is itself a violation. The Texas AG is the most aggressive state privacy enforcer in the country (Meta $1.4B, Google $1.375B, Allstate/Arity as first-ever TDPSA suit).

### Cited Findings
- TDPSA applies to entities that conduct business in Texas and process personal data and are not SBA "small businesses"; unlike Virginia/Utah/Iowa models it "has no specific thresholds based on annual revenue or volume" — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/07/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act-overview)
- Small businesses (per SBA size standards, industry-specific and "surprisingly complicated") get a partial exemption but still "cannot sell sensitive personal data without consumer consent" — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/07/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act-overview)
- TDPSA sensitive data includes racial/ethnic origin, religion, health diagnosis, sexual orientation, citizenship and immigration status, genetic/biometric data, precise geolocation, and children's data; processing sensitive data without consent is prohibited — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/07/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act-overview)
- TDPSA consent must be "a clear affirmative act" that is freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous; consent via general ToS acceptance, hovering, or dark patterns is expressly invalid — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/07/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act-overview)
- Controllers that sell sensitive data must post verbatim: "NOTICE: We may sell your sensitive personal data" (parallel notice for biometric data) — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/07/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act-overview)
- Data protection assessments required for targeted advertising, sales, profiling, sensitive-data processing, and heightened-risk processing; assessments done for comparable state laws satisfy TDPSA — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/07/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act-overview)
- Enforcement: exclusive Texas AG authority, up to $7,500/violation, permanent (non-sunsetting) 30-day cure period, no private right of action — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/07/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act-overview); the AG maintains a TDPSA consumer-complaint portal — [Texas AG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/consumer-protection/file-consumer-complaint/consumer-privacy-rights/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act)
- Jan 2025: Texas AG sued Allstate and its subsidiary Arity — the first-ever enforcement action under any state comprehensive privacy law — alleging: no clear privacy notice for geolocation collection; no affirmative consent for precise geolocation (sensitive data); failure to post the "NOTICE: We may sell your sensitive personal data" disclosure; and no opt-out mechanism for sales/targeted advertising. Data was collected via SDKs embedded in third-party apps (Life360, GasBuddy, Fuel Rewards, Routely) covering 45M+ Americans — [Vinson & Elkins](https://www.velaw.com/insights/texas-ag-targets-allstate-in-first-enforcement-of-texas-data-privacy-and-security-act/); [Texas AG press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-sues-allstate-and-arity-unlawfully-collecting-using-and-selling-over-45)
- Broader posture: Paxton launched a dedicated Data Privacy and Security Initiative/enforcement team in June 2024; Meta settled for $1.4B (July 2024, CUBI facial recognition) and Google for $1.375B (May 2025, facial/voice biometrics); the Allstate TDPSA case remained in early-stage litigation as of the article — [ABA, The Brief, Winter 2026](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/tort_trial_insurance_practice/resources/brief/2026-winter/everythings-bigger-texas-including-its-data-privacy-initiative/)

### Inferences
- How the small-business exemption is "lost": (a) outgrow SBA size standards for your NAICS code (for software, typically an annual-receipts test), at which point the full TDPSA applies; or (b) even while small, "sell" (broadly, exchange for any valuable consideration) sensitive data without prior consent, which is directly enforceable against small businesses. Sharing user data with an LLM API vendor under a proper processor/service-provider agreement is processing, not sale; sharing with any partner for their own use (analytics SDKs, ad networks, "insights" deals) risks being a sale of sensitive data.
- Build requirement regardless of exemption: opt-in consent screen (clear affirmative act, unbundled from ToS) BEFORE processing immigration status in the cloud; no sale of any user data, stated flatly in the policy; never post the "we may sell" notice because Duly should never sell.
- The Allstate complaint is a template of exactly what the Texas AG looks for: notice, sensitive-data consent, sale disclosures, opt-out mechanisms. Duly's SDK hygiene matters: embedding third-party analytics/ad SDKs that siphon data is the fact pattern that got Arity's partners named.

### Gaps
- No reliable confirmation of an Allstate/Arity settlement amount. A blog (tdpsa.org) references a "$1 billion" settlement narrative, but no primary source (AG press release, court filing) or major law firm confirmed a settlement; the ABA Winter 2026 piece still describes the case as pending. Treat settlement claims as unverified.
- Exact SBA size standard for Duly's NAICS code (e.g., 513210 software publishers, ~$47M receipts) not verified this session; confirm against the current SBA table.

## Key Question 3: Other states treating immigration/citizenship status as sensitive; strictest-common-denominator; DPIA triggers

### Takeaway
"Citizenship or immigration status" is a standard sensitive-data category in nearly every Virginia-model state law (VA, CO, CT, TX, OR, MT, DE, NJ, NE, MN, TN, IN, KY, RI, FL and others), all requiring opt-in consent; California is the outlier that added it by amendment (AB 947) with an opt-out/limit model. Maryland MODPA is the strictest: sensitive data collection limited to what is "strictly necessary" for the requested service, and sale of sensitive data banned outright even with consent. Building to MODPA + TDPSA (opt-in, strictly-necessary minimization, no sale ever, DPIAs on file) satisfies effectively every state.

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 state-law comparison chart lists citizenship/immigration status in the sensitive-data definitions of Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, Iowa, Texas, Montana, Oregon, Florida, Delaware, Nebraska, and others, with opt-in consent for sensitive data in all Virginia-model states (California opt-out); 2025-26 laws in Tennessee, Indiana, Kentucky, Rhode Island follow the Virginia model including immigration status — [Recording Law comparison chart](https://www.recordinglaw.com/us-laws/data-privacy-laws/us-state-privacy-laws-comparison/) (chart also lists some states of doubtful status, e.g. Vermont/Oklahoma/Alabama — see Gaps)
- Maryland MODPA: effective Oct 1, 2025 (applies to processing on/after Apr 1, 2026); thresholds 35,000+ MD residents, or 10,000+ plus 20%+ revenue from data sales; sensitive data includes "citizenship or immigration status" and national origin — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/privacy-and-data-security/now-in-effect-maryland-law-raises-bar-on-sensitive-data-data-minimization-and-children-s-privacy)
- MODPA data minimization: sensitive data may not be collected beyond what is "strictly necessary to provide or maintain a specific product or service" — consent does not cure over-collection — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/privacy-and-data-security/now-in-effect-maryland-law-raises-bar-on-sensitive-data-data-minimization-and-children-s-privacy)
- MODPA bans sale of sensitive data "under any circumstances," even with consent — first state to do so; AG-only enforcement — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/privacy-and-data-security/now-in-effect-maryland-law-raises-bar-on-sensitive-data-data-minimization-and-children-s-privacy)
- Connecticut SB 1295 (effective July 1, 2026) lowered/changed thresholds so the CTDPA now applies to any entity that controls/processes "consumers' sensitive data" at all (or 35,000+ consumers, or offers personal data for sale), and added government-ID numbers and financial account numbers to sensitive data — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20260731-connecticut-amends-its-data-privacy-act-to-increase-data-protections)
- Thresholds for other immigration-sensitive states: Oregon and New Jersey and Minnesota and Colorado: 100,000+ consumers or 25,000+ plus data-sale revenue; Delaware: 35,000+ or 10,000+ plus sale revenue, dropping to 10,000/5,000 on Jan 1, 2027 — [Recording Law](https://www.recordinglaw.com/us-laws/data-privacy-laws/us-state-privacy-laws-comparison/)
- DPIA landscape: roughly 15 states mandate data protection assessments; common triggers are targeted advertising, sale, profiling with significant effects, sensitive-data processing, and children's data; Maryland is "one of the strictest — data minimization + DPIA"; California uniquely requires risk-assessment submission to the CPPA plus cybersecurity-audit rules; Iowa and Utah require none — [PrivacyLawMap DPIA guide](https://privacylawmap.com/blog/data-privacy-impact-assessment-state-laws-guide); see also [Kaufman Dolowich](https://www.kaufmandolowich.com/news-resources/state-data-privacy-laws-increasingly-require-risk-assessments-for-high-risk-processing-4-30-2026/)
- TDPSA DPIAs: sensitive-data processing is itself a trigger, and assessments prepared for comparable laws are reciprocally accepted — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/07/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act-overview)

### Inferences
- Duly triggers the sensitive-data DPIA prong in every DPIA state the moment it crosses that state's applicability threshold, because its entire product is sensitive-data processing. Connecticut is the sleeper: post-SB 1295, merely processing any Connecticut resident's sensitive data appears to pull a company in with no volume floor, TDPSA-style. Practical move: write ONE data protection assessment now (processing purposes, necessity/proportionality, risks, mitigations like on-device storage and encryption, LLM-vendor terms) and maintain it; reciprocity clauses make it portable across TX/CT/OR/DE/NJ/MN/MD.
- Strictest-common-denominator build target: (1) unbundled opt-in consent before any cloud processing of immigration data (TX + all VA-model states); (2) collect only what is strictly necessary for the feature the student is using (MODPA); (3) absolute no-sale policy for all personal data, since MODPA bars selling sensitive data even with consent; (4) DPIA on file; (5) GPC + Limit-link readiness for CA at scale. Meeting this bundle also satisfies the laxer states automatically.

### Gaps
- The Recording Law chart's inclusion of Vermont, Oklahoma, Alabama, Louisiana as states with comprehensive laws is inconsistent with the widely tracked enacted-law list and could not be corroborated; rely on IAPP/Bloomberg trackers ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/resources/article/us-state-privacy-laws-overview), [Bloomberg Law](https://pro.bloomberglaw.com/insights/privacy/state-privacy-legislation-tracker/)) for the authoritative enacted list before citing specific states beyond the well-established ones (VA, CO, CT, UT, IA, TX, MT, OR, FL, DE, NJ, NH, NE, MN, TN, IN, KY, RI, MD).
- Whether New Jersey's NJDPA sensitive-data list verbatim includes immigration status (it lists national origin and status as transgender/nonbinary; immigration status inclusion should be verified against N.J.S.A. 56:8-166.4 text).

## Key Question 4: Valid consent UX, rights-request timelines, deletion with client-side encryption

### Takeaway
Regulators have converged on the same consent test: unbundled, symmetrical, plain-language, revocable choices with no dark patterns, judged by effect on the user rather than designer intent (CPPA 2024 enforcement advisory; TDPSA's express rejection of ToS-bundled consent). Rights requests run on a 45-day clock (CCPA and most states, one 45-day extension); deletion of client-encrypted data is legitimately satisfied by deleting ciphertext and destroying keys, but the flow must still delete cloud-side account/checklist/query data.

### Cited Findings
- CPPA enforcement advisory (2024) requirements for valid consent: symmetry in choice (privacy-protective option cannot take more steps), easy-to-understand language, no manipulative choice architecture, low friction; "Dark patterns aren't about intent, they're about effect" — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2024/09/california-guidance-on-dark-patterns-and-privacy)
- CPPA-flagged dark patterns: opt-out flows longer than opt-in; "yes"/"ask me later" pairings with no real "no"; one acknowledgment button covering multiple data uses. Compliant: symmetric accept-all/decline-all, and separate yes/no per purpose; the agency evaluates font size, color, placement, and click paths fact-specifically — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2024/09/california-guidance-on-dark-patterns-and-privacy)
- TDPSA invalidates consent obtained through acceptance of general terms of service, hovering/pausing over content, or dark patterns; requires a freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous affirmative act — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/07/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act-overview)
- CCPA statutory/regulatory text governing rights-request handling (45-day response window with permitted extension) and notice-at-collection is consolidated in the CPPA's current statute/regs text — [CPPA statute/regs PDF](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_statute_eff_20260101.pdf)
- GPC opt-out signals must be honored automatically without a separate consumer request — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/guides/website-disclaimers/limit-the-use-of-my-sensitive-personal-information/)

### Inferences
- Build-ready consent flow for Duly: at onboarding, a dedicated consent screen (separate from ToS/account creation) that (a) names the sensitive categories in plain words ("your visa type, status dates, and related records"), (b) states the specific purposes (deadline tracking, checklist generation, answering your questions), (c) offers equal-weight Agree / Don't agree buttons where declining still lets the user use on-device-only features, (d) is re-presented per new purpose (e.g., a later opt-in if AI queries start including document text), and (e) is revocable in settings with one tap, with revocation stopping cloud processing prospectively. Log consent events (timestamp, screen version) as evidence.
- Rights plumbing to ship: in-app "Download my data" (access/portability), "Delete my account and data" (deletion), and "Correct" affordances; a 45-day SLA tracker with identity verification proportionate to data sensitivity; support at least email as a second request channel. Because most of Duly's users are one identity (account holder), verification can be login-based, which the CCPA regs prefer over collecting new ID documents.
- Deletion with client-encrypted vault: document plaintext never existed server-side, so deletion = (1) hard-delete cloud rows (account, checklist state, query logs) within the SLA, (2) purge from backups on the backup rotation schedule and disclose that window in the policy, (3) on-device wipe of the local vault and keys on account deletion, (4) instruct the LLM processor to delete or confirm non-retention per its DPA. Crypto-shredding (destroying keys for any server-held ciphertext) is the standard pattern and should be documented in the DPIA.

### Gaps
- No state regulator has published guidance specifically blessing crypto-shredding as "deletion"; this is an industry-standard inference, not a cited regulatory position.
- Verbatim 45-day citation (Cal. Civ. Code §1798.130(a)(2)) not independently pulled this session; it is contained in the CPPA consolidated PDF cited above.

## Key Question 5: Privacy-engineering patterns for the hybrid architecture

### Takeaway
The legal exposure concentrates in three engineering choices: what crosses the wire to the LLM, how long cloud data lives, and whether marketing claims match the actual data flows. The FTC's active posture on AI data handling (the Sept 2025 6(b) study demands companies' data-handling and marketing-representation practices) plus Texas's Allstate suit (notice + consent + SDK data flows) define the failure modes. Claims must be scoped: "documents never leave your device" is defensible only if AI queries never carry document contents or extracted fields.

### Cited Findings
- The FTC's Sept 11, 2025 6(b) orders to seven AI-companion companies demand information on marketing claims/"representations about capabilities and limitations," data handling, monetization, monitoring for negative impacts, and minors' protections — signaling that data-handling practices and advertising claims of consumer AI apps are under direct FTC scrutiny — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2025/09/ftc-ai-chatbots); [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions); [FTC 6(b) order page](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/6b-orders-file-special-report-regarding-advertising-safety-data-handling-practices-companies)
- The Allstate/Arity suit turned on undisclosed data flows through embedded SDKs and absent consent/notice, not on a breach — collection architecture itself was the violation — [Vinson & Elkins](https://www.velaw.com/insights/texas-ag-targets-allstate-in-first-enforcement-of-texas-data-privacy-and-security-act/)
- MODPA's strictly-necessary standard legally caps collection at what the specific feature requires, independent of consent — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/privacy-and-data-security/now-in-effect-maryland-law-raises-bar-on-sensitive-data-data-minimization-and-children-s-privacy)
- CCPA permitted-purpose scoping (service delivery, security, legal compliance) is what keeps SPI processing out of the "Limit" regime — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/guides/website-disclaimers/limit-the-use-of-my-sensitive-personal-information/)

### Inferences (build patterns, grounded in the above)
- Data-flow inventory first: classify every field as (a) on-device only (document images/PDFs, extracted I-20/visa numbers), (b) cloud-necessary (account email, hashed auth, checklist item IDs and completion booleans, deadline dates), (c) LLM-transient (the question text plus minimum structured context). Keep immigration-status semantics out of tier (b) where possible: store checklist state as opaque template IDs, not "F-1 OPT STEM extension pending" strings.
- Minimization before LLM calls: strip names, SEVIS/A-numbers, passport numbers, and document text client-side before the prompt leaves the device; send visa class + dates only when the question needs them; contract with the LLM vendor for zero training use and zero/short retention (a processor DPA), and disclose the vendor category in the notice.
- Retention schedule (write it down; it is asked for in every DPIA): AI query logs 30-90 days then delete or fully de-identify; checklist/account data for account life + short grace period; consent records for statute-of-limitations length; deleted accounts purged from backups within a stated backup-rotation window.
- Honest claim-scoping: the marketing claim should be two-part and literal — documents and their contents stay on your device; questions you ask the assistant, and your account and checklist data, are processed on our servers and by our AI provider under contract. An unqualified "your data never leaves your device" while running cloud AI queries is a textbook §5 deception risk given the FTC's stated focus on AI capability/limitation representations.
- Government-request policy (draft posture): publish a law-enforcement request policy stating (1) document vault contents are client-side encrypted and the server cannot produce plaintext — Duly can only produce ciphertext and the cloud-side metadata it actually holds; (2) requests require valid legal process, are reviewed and narrowed, and users are notified unless legally barred; (3) enumerate exactly which categories exist server-side so the policy doubles as a transparency statement. This matters acutely for an immigration-adjacent product whose users fear DHS/ICE data demands; minimizing what the server holds is the only reliable protection, because whatever exists server-side is subpoenaable.
- No third-party ad/analytics SDKs in the app, or only privacy-scrubbed, first-party analytics: the Arity fact pattern shows SDK-mediated flows are where sensitive-data "sale" allegations originate; an app whose install alone signals "likely non-citizen" makes even device identifiers de facto sensitive.

### Gaps
- No regulator guidance found specifically addressing client-side-encryption apps' obligations under state privacy laws (e.g., whether ciphertext held server-side is "personal data" for rights requests); treated here by the conservative assumption that it is.
- FTC "Operation AI Comply" enforcement details (2024-2025 sweep against deceptive AI claims) were not fetched this session; worth one follow-up citation if the report needs a named §5 AI-claims action.

## Key Question 6: CA SB 243 (companion chatbots), SB 942 AI Transparency Act, FTC AI posture

### Takeaway
Duly's task-oriented visa assistant is almost certainly OUT of SB 243's scope: the law targets "companion chatbots" that sustain relationship-like interactions meeting social needs, and expressly excludes bots used solely for customer service/business operations. SB 942 (AI detection/provenance, now effective Aug 2, 2026 via AB 853) targets generative image/video/audio providers with 1M+ monthly users and does not reach a small text assistant. The cheap, prudent move anyway: a persistent "AI, not a lawyer or a human" disclosure, which also serves UPL risk management and FTC honesty norms.

### Cited Findings
- SB 243 defines companion chatbots as AI with natural-language interfaces providing "adaptive, human-like responses," capable of sustaining relationships across interactions and meeting users' social needs; it excludes bots "used solely for customer service," business operations, video-game discussion, and standalone devices — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law)
- SB 243 in-scope duties: disclose AI status where a reasonable person could be misled; for minors, an AI reminder every three hours and a statement that companion chatbots "may not be suitable for some minors"; suicide/self-harm safeguards with crisis referrals; annual reporting to CA Office of Suicide Prevention starting July 1, 2027; private right of action with minimum $1,000/violation plus fees — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law); bill text — [CA Legislature SB 243](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB243)
- SB 942 applies to generative AI providers with "over 1,000,000 monthly visitors or users" publicly accessible in California; requires a free AI detection tool, disclosure options and latent provenance for AI-generated image/video/audio; AB 853 delayed the operative date from Jan 1, 2026 to Aug 2, 2026 and added phased duties (large platforms preserve provenance Jan 1, 2027; capture devices Jan 1, 2028); penalties $5,000/violation, each day a separate violation; statute covers image/video/audio, with text-only systems apparently outside scope — [AI Compliance Atlas](https://aicomplianceatlas.com/law/california-sb-942); bill — [CA Legislature SB 942](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB942)
- FTC: Sept 2025 6(b) inquiry into AI companion chatbots (safety, data handling, monetization, minors, marketing claims), announced the same day SB 243 passed; a formal market study that may inform future rulemaking/enforcement — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2025/09/ftc-ai-chatbots); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions)
- FPF analysis situates SB 243 within a broader wave of state chatbot legislation (useful for tracking copycat bills) — [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/understanding-the-new-wave-of-chatbot-legislation-california-sb-243-and-beyond/)

### Inferences
- Scope analysis for Duly: a visa-deadline assistant answering task-bound questions does not "sustain a relationship... meeting social needs" and fits the customer-service/business-operations exclusion. Design guardrails that keep it out of scope: no persistent persona/name encouraging emotional attachment, no open-ended companionship chat, session-scoped conversations, and marketing that frames it as a tool. If Duly ever adds an anthropomorphized always-there "buddy" persona (the memory-file framing "immigration buddy" is worth flagging to the founder), SB 243 analysis must be redone, and minors (17-year-old freshmen on F-1) would trigger the 3-hour reminders and safety-protocol duties.
- Regardless of scope: ship a first-run and persistent-footer disclosure that answers come from AI, are informational, and are not legal advice from an attorney. This simultaneously addresses SB 243's reasonable-person test, FTC deception norms on AI representations, and unauthorized-practice-of-law exposure.
- SB 942 is a non-issue at Duly's scale and modality (text), but note pending SB 1000 would remove the 1M-user threshold; re-check if Duly generates document images or filled forms rendered as images.
